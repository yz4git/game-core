## Observation → Mechanism → Generalized Principle → Transfer → Playtest Question

### 1. Encounter pacing is a time series, not an average difficulty
**Observation:** A generated floor can have acceptable mean enemy power while clustering danger into an exhausting or trivial sequence.
**Mechanism:** Players experience challenge in order; local peaks, recovery windows and change rate determine tension.
**Generalized Principle:** Validate generated difficulty as a temporal/spatial signal, not one scalar.
**Transfer:** Sample route segments into threat/resource/recovery values and compare their curve shape.
**Playtest Question:** Do two seeds with the same mean difficulty create clearly different fatigue because their peaks and recovery windows differ?

### 2. Pacing signatures should encode transitions
**Observation:** Counts of combat/rest/loot rooms cannot distinguish `combat-combat-rest` from `combat-rest-combat`.
**Mechanism:** Transition order changes anticipation and recovery.
**Generalized Principle:** Procedural QA should measure event transitions and run lengths as well as totals.
**Transfer:** Store encounter-type n-grams, consecutive-pressure length, peak spacing and recovery spacing.
**Playtest Question:** Are undesirable long pressure streaks visible in telemetry before human review?

### 3. Difficulty should be conditioned on player skill
**Observation:** Survival-analysis work shows difficulty curves change when player skill and learning differ.
**Mechanism:** The same obstacle distribution produces different hazard rates for different policies.
**Generalized Principle:** A procedural difficulty metric is incomplete without a player/agent model.
**Transfer:** Run novice, baseline and expert agents and compare death/hazard curves per seed.
**Playtest Question:** Does a seed remain reasonable across target skill bands, or is it only balanced for the benchmark agent?

### 4. Generator evaluation needs distributions, not showcase seeds
**Observation:** Expressive-range methods were developed partly to reduce cherry-picked PCG evaluation.
**Mechanism:** Rare failures and dense repetitive clusters disappear when only attractive examples are inspected.
**Generalized Principle:** Judge generators by coverage, density, tails and outliers across cohorts.
**Transfer:** CI reports descriptor histograms plus worst-N and nearest-duplicate clusters.
**Playtest Question:** Is the advertised variety representative of generated output or concentrated in rare seeds?

### 5. Automated metrics need human calibration
**Observation:** Empirical PCG research found computational metrics do not reliably replace user judgments.
**Mechanism:** A proxy can optimize measurable structure while missing perceived repetition, fairness or fun.
**Generalized Principle:** Every production PCG metric should periodically prove correlation with the human experience it claims to represent.
**Transfer:** Blind-rate seed pairs for repetition/readability/pacing and fit thresholds against those labels.
**Playtest Question:** Does crossing the automated threshold actually predict a noticeable human difference?

### 6. Landmark value depends on decision usefulness
**Observation:** A visually unique object may be memorable yet useless for navigation if it is not visible near meaningful choices.
**Mechanism:** Navigation requires associating a stable cue with location, direction or route choice.
**Generalized Principle:** Measure landmark usefulness by visibility × uniqueness × decision proximity.
**Transfer:** For each junction, ray/sample visible landmark IDs and score uniqueness and angular separation.
**Playtest Question:** At important forks, can players identify a stable cue that distinguishes the alternatives?

### 7. Landmark uniqueness should be local before global
**Observation:** Two landmarks may be globally different but look nearly identical from the approach angle that matters.
**Mechanism:** Players compare what is simultaneously or recently visible, not asset database identity.
**Generalized Principle:** Readability metrics should use player-view silhouettes/features at decision points.
**Transfer:** Render canonical approach thumbnails or extract coarse silhouette descriptors from junction viewpoints.
**Playtest Question:** Can players tell adjacent route cues apart before committing to the turn?

### 8. Readability failure can be measured as route ambiguity
**Observation:** Multiple paths with similar geometry and cues increase navigation uncertainty.
**Mechanism:** Equivalent-looking branches weaken the mapping between perception and route identity.
**Generalized Principle:** Procedural readability is partly the separability of choices.
**Transfer:** At junctions compare branch heading, width, lighting class, landmark signature and destination role; flag low-separation forks.
**Playtest Question:** Which junctions generate repeated wrong turns despite technically valid connectivity?

### 9. Heatmaps should be segmented by strategy, not only aggregated
**Observation:** A single occupancy heatmap can hide two distinct viable strategies by averaging them together.
**Mechanism:** Population aggregation collapses multimodal behavior.
**Generalized Principle:** Cluster traces before interpreting route heatmaps.
**Transfer:** Cluster decision signatures, then emit one occupancy/transition map per strategy cluster.
**Playtest Question:** Are there genuinely multiple strategies, or one dominant route plus noisy deviations?

### 10. Multi-agent diversity means response diversity
**Observation:** Multi-agent research distinguishes useful behavioural diversity from merely different trajectories.
**Mechanism:** A strategy matters when it creates different responses to opponents/environment and expands the effective strategy set.
**Generalized Principle:** Measure procedural strategy diversity by counterfactual effectiveness, not trace distance alone.
**Transfer:** Replay agents/policies across the same seed/opponent matrix and compare outcome vectors.
**Playtest Question:** Do two apparently different strategies succeed and fail against different situations?

### 11. Local modular generation can improve out-of-distribution robustness
**Observation:** Multi-agent PCGRL research reports improved generalization to unseen map shapes, attributed to local modular policies.
**Mechanism:** Local generation responsibilities reduce dependence on one fixed global canvas.
**Generalized Principle:** For generators expected to handle changing map dimensions/topologies, prefer composable local constraints plus global validation.
**Transfer:** Partition generation by regions/roles, then run connectivity, pacing and landmark gates globally.
**Playtest Question:** Does changing aspect ratio or map size collapse quality even when local motifs remain valid?

### 12. Enemy-power heatmaps are useful but need route weighting
**Observation:** Neon Chrome publicly demonstrates enemy power as a spatial heatmap during generation.
**Mechanism:** Spatial visualization exposes hot/cold rooms, but unreachable or rarely chosen rooms should not weigh equally.
**Generalized Principle:** Weight encounter-pressure maps by actual traversal probability.
**Transfer:** Multiply threat field by agent/player occupancy and compute experienced pressure along routes.
**Playtest Question:** Are the hottest generated encounters actually experienced, and at what point in a run?

### 13. Recovery budget should be explicit
**Observation:** Generated sequences can be individually fair yet collectively exhausting when resource and low-pressure opportunities fail to follow peaks.
**Mechanism:** Consecutive demand accumulates error/fatigue faster than players can recover.
**Generalized Principle:** Pacing validators need a recovery budget in addition to a danger ceiling.
**Transfer:** After high-pressure segments require bounded access to time, health/resource opportunity, safer space or lower input density.
**Playtest Question:** After a peak, how long until a typical player regains enough margin for the next one?

### 14. Regression should compare pacing-shape distributions
**Observation:** Generator changes can preserve mean difficulty while changing the frequency of spikes, streaks or dead zones.
**Mechanism:** Aggregate averages conceal distributional drift in sequence structure.
**Generalized Principle:** Version-to-version PCG regression should compare signature distributions, not only validity rates.
**Transfer:** Track peak count, peak spacing, pressure-run length, recovery-run length, transition entropy and route-weighted hazard distributions.
**Playtest Question:** Did a generator update silently make most runs flatter, spikier or more exhausting?

## Promoted principles

- Generated difficulty is a sequence experienced by modeled players, not a scalar attached to a seed.
- Landmark/readability metrics must be evaluated from decision-relevant player viewpoints.
- Aggregate heatmaps can hide strategy diversity; cluster behavior first.
- Automated PCG metrics require recurring human calibration.
- Recovery opportunity is a first-class pacing constraint.

## Implementation-ready QA schema

Per seed record:
- validity / solvability
- agent skill profile
- encounter sequence
- threat curve
- resource/recovery curve
- peak count and spacing
- longest pressure streak
- transition entropy / encounter n-grams
- route cluster ID
- route-weighted threat heatmap
- junction ambiguity score
- visible landmark IDs per decision point
- landmark local-separation score
- strategy outcome vector

Population dashboard:
- descriptor coverage/density
- strategy-cluster occupancy
- pacing-signature clusters
- nearest repetition clusters
- worst-N ambiguity / pressure / recovery seeds
- human-rating correlation by metric version
