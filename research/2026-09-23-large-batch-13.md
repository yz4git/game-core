# A. Replay architecture

## A1. Deterministic replay is not always the best debugging tool

### Observation
A rewindable state buffer can be more useful for immediate debugging than rebuilding the full past from deterministic inputs.

### Mechanism
Different use cases value different things:
- small files
- exact competition result
- random access
- rewind
- inspectable state

### Principle
**Choose replay architecture by guarantee, not ideology.**

---

## A2. Hybrid recording reduces the cost of seeking and divergence

Input/event logs can be combined with periodic key state snapshots.

### Principle
**Use sparse authoritative checkpoints to bound replay drift and seek time.**

---

## A3. Find the first divergence, not the final wrong result

A long replay that ends incorrectly may have diverged thousands of ticks earlier.

### Principle
Record state checksums at intervals and compare the first mismatch.

---

## A4. Version replay data like save data

Changes to physics, AI, maps and generators can invalidate a replay.

### Principle
**Replay compatibility must be explicit, not accidental.**

---

# B. iPhone touch as a native input system

## B1. Safe area is also an input reliability area

Apple guidance explicitly calls out avoiding the Home indicator, Dynamic Island and device boundaries for important virtual controls.

### Principle
**Do not place critical actions where system interaction can compete with game input.**

---

## B2. Input surface can be much larger than visual control

Touch has no tactile thumbstick boundary.

### Principle
Use broad hit regions—potentially a screen half—for movement/camera while rendering only lightweight visual feedback.

---

## B3. Simultaneous actions must be assigned by finger

A controller can support combinations that a two-thumb touch layout cannot comfortably reproduce.

### Principle
**Map simultaneous-action relationships before placing touch buttons.**

---

## B4. Haptics should form a consistent causal language

Apple recommends consistent relationships between haptic patterns and actions.

### Principle
Treat haptic sharpness/intensity/pattern as an information vocabulary.

---

# C. UGC as versioned, auditable content

## C1. A report must identify exactly what was reported

Moderation evidence needs the content, user, type, reason, timestamp and origin.

### Principle
**Report state must survive later content edits.**

---

## C2. Reporters should not need to repeatedly see reported content

Hide/block controls can act immediately while formal moderation is pending.

---

## C3. Published UGC needs version IDs

If a map changes after:
- rating
- leaderboard
- replay
- report
then those records become ambiguous.

### Principle
**Pin derived records to the exact published version.**

---

## C4. Remix needs provenance

A remix system should preserve parent content and version relationships.

### Principle
Treat remix lineage as structured data.

---

## C5. Automatic attribution is necessary but socially incomplete

Research on remix communities suggests system-generated attribution and voluntary human credit can carry different social meaning.

### Principle
Keep automatic provenance and also allow explicit creator acknowledgment.

---

# D. Returning-player calibration

## D1. Old rating is an uncertain measurement

Long inactivity does not prove skill decline; it makes the old estimate less certain.

### Principle
**Decay confidence faster than rating.**

---

## D2. Recalibration should adapt both directions

Returning players may be:
- rusty
- unchanged
- stronger due to adjacent experience.

### Principle
Do not assume one fixed direction of skill change.

---

## D3. Warm-up and competitive calibration are separate

A player can remember controls in practice before skill rating needs to move.

### Principle
Provide a low-stakes re-entry path before ranked pressure.

---

## D4. Season reset is not skill reset

Visible rank, reward track, MMR and uncertainty are separate systems and can reset differently.

---

# E. Procedural regression infrastructure

## E1. Keep canonical and exploratory seed pools

Canonical catches regressions.
Random exploration finds new failures.

### Principle
**Every discovered procedural failure becomes future test data.**

---

## E2. Separate hard validity from soft quality

A map can be valid and still be unpleasant.

Hard:
- connectivity
- invalid geometry
- NaN

Soft:
- braking
- repetition
- poor landmark visibility

---

## E3. Monitor distribution tails

Procedural systems often fail in rare cases.

### Principle
Track worst seeds and high percentiles, not just averages.

---

## E4. Trace generation decisions

Seed reproduction identifies the output.
Generation trace helps identify the reason.

### Principle
Store enough generator decisions to compare old and new behavior.
