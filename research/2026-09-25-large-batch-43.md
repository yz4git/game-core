# Large Batch 43 — Human-confirmed failure → adaptive procedural search priors

Date: 2026-09-25

## Scope
Follow-up selected from `research/COVERAGE.md`: learn mutation/operator priors from human-confirmed failures without letting the learned policy redefine validity or collapse exploration.

Historical cross-sample: Gaming Alexandria preserved programs from Micom BASIC / Program Pochette across 1983–1996, including Fuwarp Fuwarp, PYON PYON, Xemious, Metal Arms, BATTLE BALLER, Ball & Hole, Alien, Thief, Let Out, Missile & Missile, The 29, Wave, Star Fighter and Eat Eat. These compact games expose different failure budgets: finite lives/ammo, acceleration and stopping distance, time pressure, pursuit zones, irreversible puzzle motion, escalating density and restart/continue cadence.

Related public research: adaptive operator selection with bandits and sliding-window credit, dynamic Thompson sampling for non-stationary operator rewards, classification-tree operator prediction from historical offspring, active-learning test selection, and human-oracle active learning.

No magazine prose/images are reproduced. The items below are independent abstractions for implementation and playtest use.

## New observations / mechanisms

### 1. Human confirmation should train search, not legality
**Observation:** Human review is valuable evidence about which valid generated cases matter, but reviewers should not silently redefine the generator's legal-content contract.
**Mechanism:** Feed labels only after sacred-invariant and validity gates.
**Generalized Principle:** Learn where to search inside the valid space; keep validity independently specified.
**Transfer:** PCG levels, road worlds, encounters, puzzles.
**Playtest Question:** Can a learned prior ever increase the probability of invalid content bypassing the gate?

### 2. Operator usefulness is contextual
**Observation:** A mutation can be productive in one topology/phase and useless in another.
**Mechanism:** Condition credit on genre, generator epoch, behavior cell, topology, decision phase and margin region.
**Generalized Principle:** Prefer contextual priors over one global operator score.
**Transfer:** Hairpin curvature vs straight roads; boss pressure vs recovery rooms.
**Playtest Question:** Which contexts reverse the ranking of mutation operators?

### 3. Reward actionable novelty, not duplicate bug volume
**Observation:** Rediscovering one common failure can dominate naive success counts.
**Mechanism:** Decay reward for duplicate signatures; reward severity × reproducibility × actionable novelty.
**Generalized Principle:** Search yield is information gained, not failures counted.
**Transfer:** Crash fuzzing, fairness QA, procedural visual defects.
**Playtest Question:** Is the scheduler learning to farm one easy failure family?

### 4. Preserve an exploration floor
**Observation:** Exploiting historically successful operators can make rare new failure families invisible.
**Mechanism:** Minimum probability/budget per operator family plus unbiased random/canonical floors.
**Generalized Principle:** Learned priors allocate the adaptive remainder, never the whole QA budget.
**Transfer:** Mutation scheduling and parent selection.
**Playtest Question:** Could every supported operator still be sampled this release?

### 5. Priors must decay across semantic drift
**Observation:** Generator/ruleset/agent changes alter operator reward distributions.
**Mechanism:** Version priors; use sliding windows/decay; reset strongly on descriptor or validity-contract changes.
**Generalized Principle:** Treat procedural search as non-stationary.
**Transfer:** Live generators, balance patches, physics changes.
**Playtest Question:** Which dependency change invalidates old operator credit?

### 6. Separate parent selection from mutation selection
**Observation:** A good operator applied to an uninformative parent can look bad, and vice versa.
**Mechanism:** Log and learn two policies: region/parent selection and mutation/operator selection.
**Generalized Principle:** Do not confound where to search with how to perturb.
**Transfer:** MAP-Elites archives, seed corpora, scenario mutation.
**Playtest Question:** Did yield improve because of better parents or better mutations?

### 7. Human false positives are useful negative evidence
**Observation:** Automated severe cases sometimes look harmless to players.
**Mechanism:** Preserve rejected cases with context and reason; update precision calibration rather than deleting them.
**Generalized Principle:** Human disagreement is training data for triage quality.
**Transfer:** Visibility, difficulty, fairness, aesthetic defects.
**Playtest Question:** Which machine predicates repeatedly overestimate human severity?

### 8. Severity is cohort-dependent
**Observation:** A failure boundary for novices may be comfortable for experts, while exploit-seeking experts expose other failures.
**Mechanism:** Store reviewer/player cohort with labels; estimate separate severity/precision by cohort.
**Generalized Principle:** Avoid collapsing human confirmation into one universal scalar.
**Transfer:** Shooter reaction slack, racing recovery, puzzle knowledge, RPG resource debt.
**Playtest Question:** Does the learned scheduler erase novice-only or expert-only failures?

### 9. Query humans where labels change policy most
**Observation:** Human review is expensive; obvious duplicates provide little learning value.
**Mechanism:** Prioritize uncertain, high-severity, policy-disagreement and novel-signature candidates for review.
**Generalized Principle:** Use active review to reduce uncertainty, not to rubber-stamp automation.
**Transfer:** CI triage and nightly adversarial campaigns.
**Playtest Question:** How many reviewed cases actually changed ranking/calibration?

### 10. Learn compound mutations sparsely
**Observation:** Many real failures arise from two individually acceptable margins becoming tight together.
**Mechanism:** Promote promising operator pairs only after single-operator evidence; maintain pair-specific credit.
**Generalized Principle:** Search interactions selectively instead of enumerating all combinations.
**Transfer:** road curvature × traffic, enemy timing × occlusion, resource debt × status effects.
**Playtest Question:** Which operator pairs discover failures neither operator finds alone?

### 11. Regression-test the search policy itself
**Observation:** Higher average yield can hide loss of a rare severe failure family.
**Mechanism:** Fixed reachable-failure corpus; compare time-to-first, family recall, human-confirmed yield, invalid rate, diversity and compute cost before scheduler rollout.
**Generalized Principle:** The bug finder is production software and needs regression tests.
**Transfer:** bandit schedulers, learned selectors, active-learning triage.
**Playtest Question:** Did a scheduler update improve throughput while reducing severe-family recall?

### 12. Store provenance sufficient to replay learning decisions
**Observation:** A learned prior is hard to debug without the evidence that changed it.
**Mechanism:** Persist context, parent, operator, semantic delta, repair delta, margins, signature, machine severity, human label/cohort, cost and policy version.
**Generalized Principle:** Adaptive QA needs auditable credit assignment.
**Transfer:** release dashboards and failure shrinking.
**Playtest Question:** Can a surprising operator probability be traced to concrete reviewed cases?

## Production pipeline
`VALIDITY CONTRACT → CONTEXT/PARENT POLICY → OPERATOR POLICY → SEMANTIC MUTATION → VALIDITY/REPAIR → MARGIN + SIGNATURE → SELECTIVE HUMAN QUERY → COHORT LABEL → CONTEXTUAL CREDIT UPDATE → POLICY REGRESSION CORPUS`

## Minimum telemetry
Per context/operator: attempts, valid rate, repair distance, novel signatures, severe signatures, duplicate rate, human-confirmed precision/recall proxy, cohort, compute cost, time-to-first actionable failure, and policy version.
