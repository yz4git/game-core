# Large Batch 41 — Cost-Bounded Procedural QA and CI Sharding

Date: 2026-09-24

## Scope

This batch follows `research/COVERAGE.md` and targets a remaining high-priority gap: **production cost budgets for procedural/adversarial QA** — seeds/hour, agent CPU, render/storage cost, staged evaluation, sharding, and evidence-per-compute stopping rules.

Historical cross-sample used preserved small games from Gaming Alexandria across 1983–1996: Micom BASIC `Xemious` (1983), `Fuwarp Fuwarp` (1983), `PYON PYON` (1984), `Excite Race` (1985), `Ball & Hole` (1986), `Let Out` (1989), `Metal Arms` (1990), and `BATTLE BALLER` (1996), plus Program Pochette `The 29`, `The UFO`, `Popping Crusher`, `Thief`, and `Alien`. These are used only as compact design evidence: short lives, finite shots, timers, acceleration, course time trials, escalating density, pursuit AI, and restart/continue loops expose different player-budget and simulation-cost shapes without preserving magazine text or imagery.

Modern/public material cross-checked:

- Mariño, Reis & Lelis (AIIDE 2015), Mario metric/user-study comparison: computational metrics are useful but cannot simply replace human evaluation.

## Observation 1 — A seed count is not a QA budget

**Observation:** Two seeds can differ by orders of magnitude in solver expansions, simulated frames, policy count, rendering, and artifact size.

**Mechanism:** Fixed `N seeds` silently allocates most compute to pathological long cases and makes releases incomparable when generator complexity changes.

**Generalized Principle:** Budget procedural QA in **resource units and evidence units**, not seed count alone.

**Transfer:** Record CPU-ms, simulated frames, solver expansions, GPU-ms, bytes written, and wall-clock per stage; normalize discoveries by compute-hour.

**Playtest Question:** Did this release test more meaningful behavior, or merely execute more cheap seeds?

## Observation 2 — Cheap predicates should buy expensive simulations

**Observation:** Reachability, bounds, coarse clearance, dependency order, and simple margin estimates are much cheaper than full agent simulation/rendering.

**Mechanism:** If every candidate receives the expensive path, obvious duplicates and trivial invalidity consume the same budget as informative near-boundary cases.

**Generalized Principle:** Treat QA as a **cost-escalation funnel**; a candidate earns the next stage by adding expected information.

**Transfer:** `STATIC → CHEAP AGENT → FULL AGENT → MULTI-POLICY → RENDER → HUMAN`. Promote by novelty, severity, uncertainty, disagreement, or regression relevance.

**Playtest Question:** What percentage of full simulations could have been rejected or deduplicated by a cheaper gate?

## Observation 3 — Cap pathological cases without erasing them

**Observation:** Hard puzzles, stuck navigation, oscillating AI, or endless combat can monopolize a shard.

**Mechanism:** A timeout treated as ordinary discard hides exactly the cases most likely to reveal a complexity or soft-lock failure.

**Generalized Principle:** Use **stage-specific work caps**, but classify cap exhaustion as evidence.

**Transfer:** Save `TIMEOUT/EXPANSION_CAP/FRAME_CAP/MEMORY_CAP` as explicit failure signatures with partial traces and margins; route a small quota to extended-budget replay.

**Playtest Question:** Are expensive cases disappearing from reports because they timed out?

## Observation 4 — Shard by predicted cost, not seed range

**Observation:** Hash/range sharding can place several extreme cases on one worker while others finish early.

**Mechanism:** Heavy-tail simulation cost creates stragglers and poor machine utilization.

**Generalized Principle:** Balance CI shards by **estimated work**, then use work stealing for residual variance.

**Transfer:** Maintain a lightweight cost predictor from static descriptors and recent telemetry; bin-pack candidates by predicted CPU/GPU time rather than numeric seed intervals.

**Playtest Question:** Is CI completion dominated by one straggler shard?

## Observation 5 — Cache deterministic evidence at the right boundary

**Observation:** Generator, navigation, simulation, rendering, and scoring do not all change every commit.

**Mechanism:** Re-running unchanged expensive stages wastes budget; over-broad caching risks stale conclusions.

**Generalized Principle:** Cache by **semantic dependency fingerprint**, not by seed alone.

**Transfer:** Key artifacts by generator version + ruleset + collision/nav + agent policy + descriptor schema + renderer where applicable. Invalidate only downstream stages whose dependencies changed.

**Playtest Question:** Which expensive result was recomputed even though none of its semantic inputs changed?

## Observation 6 — Preserve a fixed evidence floor before adaptive search

**Observation:** Pure adaptive/adversarial allocation naturally chases newly interesting regions.

**Mechanism:** This can starve boring but important canonical and random cohorts, destroying longitudinal comparability and population estimates.

**Generalized Principle:** Reserve compute before optimization: **canonical floor + unbiased random floor + adaptive remainder**.

**Transfer:** Example policy: guarantee canonical regression completion and a minimum random sample first; spend remaining budget on frontier, disagreement, and severe-failure search. Percentages are project-calibrated, not universal constants.

**Playtest Question:** Can this release still be compared to the previous one after the adversarial search policy changed?

## Observation 7 — Allocate compute by marginal information gain

**Observation:** New behavior cells, severe failure signatures, and human-confirmed issues often arrive rapidly early and then plateau.

**Mechanism:** Continuing a saturated cohort has diminishing return while another cohort may still discover useful cases.

**Generalized Principle:** Schedule the next unit of compute where **expected evidence gain per cost** is highest, subject to coverage floors.

**Transfer:** Track rolling `new behavior cells / CPU-hour`, `new severe signatures / CPU-hour`, `human-confirmed issues / render-hour`, and uncertainty reduction. Reallocate only after repeated windows, not one noisy dip.

**Playtest Question:** Which cohort currently produces the most new actionable evidence per compute-hour?

## Observation 8 — Separate discovery from confirmation

**Observation:** Cheap approximate agents are good at finding suspicious cases but may mis-rank human difficulty or fairness.

**Mechanism:** Using expensive high-fidelity policies everywhere is unaffordable; trusting cheap proxies everywhere creates false confidence.

**Generalized Principle:** Use **cheap search, expensive confirmation**.

**Transfer:** Search broadly with static/approximate policies; confirm top severity, high uncertainty, policy disagreement, and boundary cases with high-fidelity simulation and sampled human play.

**Playtest Question:** Is the expensive tier validating hypotheses, or redundantly rediscovering what cheap tiers already know?

## Observation 9 — Artifact retention needs a value hierarchy

**Observation:** RGB/video, depth, semantic buffers, traces, and checkpoints can exceed simulation cost in storage and transfer.

**Mechanism:** Keeping every artifact makes large seed campaigns operationally unsustainable; keeping only pass/fail destroys debuggability.

**Generalized Principle:** Retain **reproduction-critical state universally; rich evidence selectively**.

**Transfer:** Always keep seed/fingerprint/descriptors/margins/outcome/cost. Keep full traces and semantic buffers for canonical failures, novel signatures, frontier cases, and a small unbiased sample. Compress or expire redundant passing renders.

**Playtest Question:** Could a severe failure still be reproduced after its large media artifacts expire?

## Observation 10 — Render budget should follow semantic risk

**Observation:** Full visual regression is expensive and many generated cases are mechanically redundant.

**Mechanism:** Uniform screenshot/video capture spends GPU/storage on low-information cosmetic variants.

**Generalized Principle:** Render candidates selected by **semantic novelty, visibility risk, mechanical disagreement, or changed rendering dependencies**.

**Transfer:** Use collision/nav/affordance/landmark signatures first; render worst-N and representative cells, plus a small random visual audit.

**Playtest Question:** Are we paying to render many cases with the same gameplay signature?

## Observation 11 — Cost regressions are gameplay-production regressions

**Observation:** A generator update can preserve pass rate while doubling solver expansions, simulation frames, memory, or render time.

**Mechanism:** This reduces future QA breadth and may foreshadow runtime stalls or pathological content complexity.

**Generalized Principle:** Treat **evaluation-cost distributions as regression metrics**.

**Transfer:** Compare p50/p95/p99 CPU-ms, expansions, simulated frames, GPU-ms, and artifact bytes by behavior cell and failure class across builds.

**Playtest Question:** Did content remain valid only by becoming much more expensive to validate or run?

## Observation 12 — Stop by evidence saturation under a declared budget

**Observation:** “Run until no bugs” has no operational endpoint; fixed seed counts ignore evidence density.

**Mechanism:** Teams either overspend indefinitely or stop arbitrarily.

**Generalized Principle:** Release confidence should combine **minimum cohort floors + repeated evidence-saturation windows + explicit residual risk**.

**Transfer:** Stop adaptive expansion only after rolling novelty/severe-failure/disagreement gains remain below calibrated thresholds for multiple windows, canonical tests pass, unbiased floor is met, and no unresolved high-severity cluster remains. Report what was not explored.

**Playtest Question:** What new evidence would another compute-hour plausibly buy, and what risk remains if we stop now?

## Production budget model

For each candidate and stage record:

`{seed, cohort, behaviorCell, stage, cpuMs, gpuMs, simFrames, solverExpansions, peakMemory, bytesStored, outcome, failureSignature, novelty, uncertainty}`

Release-level accounting should expose:

- candidates/hour by stage, but never alone;
- CPU/GPU hours by cohort;
- new behavior cells / CPU-hour;
- severe failure signatures / CPU-hour;
- confirmed severe issues / human-review hour;
- p95/p99 case cost and timeout rate;
- render/storage bytes per retained actionable case;
- cache hit rate by semantic stage;
- shard imbalance (`max shard time / median shard time`);
- estimated cost to reproduce the full canonical suite.

## Recommended CI ladder

1. **PR smoke:** canonical subset + cheap static predicates; strict wall-clock budget.
2. **PR targeted:** impacted behavior cells and dependency-aware cached stages.
3. **Mainline:** full canonical + minimum unbiased random cohort + bounded adversarial search.
4. **Nightly:** multi-policy simulation, broader boundary search, selective semantic rendering.
5. **Scheduled deep campaign:** extended timeout replay, human-calibration sample, long-tail storage/thermal/device matrices where relevant.

A failure found in a deep tier should be shrunk into a cheaper canonical fixture whenever possible, moving future detection left.

## Cost-aware promotion score

Do not use one universal scalar for final severity, but a scheduler may estimate promotion value as:

`expected_information_gain × consequence × uncertainty / expected_cost`

Hard rules override this score: canonical regressions, known severe signatures, security/economy invariants, and sacred playability failures always receive required validation.

## Key synthesis

The production objective is not “maximum seeds.” It is **maximum trustworthy new evidence under bounded compute, render, storage, and human-review cost**. Cheap predicates create breadth; expensive simulation creates confidence; selective human review calibrates meaning. The pipeline must preserve enough unbiased sampling that adaptive search does not turn QA into a self-fulfilling hunt for only the failures it already knows how to see.
