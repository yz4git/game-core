## New observations and mechanisms

### 1. A genre objective should attack the player's dominant failure budget, not generic geometry
**Observation:** Legal geometry can still produce a catastrophically unfair shooter, racer, RPG encounter or puzzle for completely different reasons.
**Mechanism:** Each genre converts layout/content into a different scarce budget: reaction time, braking distance, ammunition/space, party contribution, information, or irreversible moves.
**Generalized Principle:** Define adversarial objectives in the units of the genre's dominant decision budget.
**Transfer:** QA descriptors should expose those units before search begins.
**Playtest Question:** What resource or margin reaches zero immediately before players call this genre unfair?

### 2. Shooter fairness is a time-to-threat and escape-space problem
**Observation:** Enemy count alone poorly predicts shooter pressure when spawn angle, occlusion, projectile travel and safe-space collapse differ.
**Mechanism:** Several individually survivable threats can synchronize so their reaction windows overlap and eliminate every escape corridor.
**Generalized Principle:** Search for minimum joint reaction slack and minimum escape-cone availability, not maximum enemy count.
**Transfer:** Measure spawn-to-threat time, simultaneous threat count, safe angular sector, cover transition distance and unavoidable-damage lower bound.
**Playtest Question:** Did the player have a readable and physically reachable response before damage became inevitable?

### 3. Shooter resource starvation must be evaluated along plausible combat traces
**Observation:** Total ammo or health placed in a level can look sufficient while being unavailable before the encounter that consumes it.
**Mechanism:** Resource topology and expected expenditure interact with route choice and accuracy.
**Generalized Principle:** Resource sufficiency is path- and policy-conditioned.
**Transfer:** Run novice/baseline/expert accuracy policies and search minimum pre-encounter ammo/health slack across reachable routes.
**Playtest Question:** Which reasonable route first forces a competent but imperfect player into resource-negative combat?

### 4. Racing validity is speed-conditioned
**Observation:** A road can be collision-free and connected but functionally invalid at the speed the game teaches the player to carry.
**Mechanism:** curvature, sight distance, braking distance, traffic and recovery room combine into a deadline.
**Generalized Principle:** Evaluate road geometry against approach-state envelopes, not stationary traversal.
**Transfer:** Search minimum `visible braking distance - required braking distance`, steering-rate saturation and post-contact recovery distance.
**Playtest Question:** Can a player following the game's normal speed language react without clairvoyance?

### 5. Racing failures often cascade through recovery denial
**Observation:** One mistake may be acceptable while the generated aftermath causes repeated collisions or traps the vehicle in a low-speed state.
**Mechanism:** traffic arrival, narrow shoulders, turn geometry and acceleration create a recovery basin.
**Generalized Principle:** Adversarial racing QA must test recovery trajectories after plausible mistakes, not only perfect laps.
**Transfer:** Inject wall contact/spin/slowdown at representative points and measure time-to-safe-speed, secondary-contact probability and lost-position tail.
**Playtest Question:** Does one ordinary mistake create a disproportionately long punishment loop?

### 6. RPG encounter difficulty is multidimensional contribution balance
**Observation:** A fight can hit a target win rate while one party role is irrelevant or one strategy dominates every generated encounter.
**Mechanism:** enemy targeting, resistances, terrain, action economy and resource carryover determine which roles contribute.
**Generalized Principle:** Optimize encounter difficulty and contribution diversity separately.
**Transfer:** Search encounters with acceptable completion probability but extreme contribution Gini/role redundancy or one-policy dominance.
**Playtest Question:** Is every intended role meaningfully useful, and are there multiple credible plans?

### 7. RPG procedural danger must include campaign-state carryover
**Observation:** An encounter that is fair from full resources can be unfair when reached after the generator's preceding sequence.
**Mechanism:** HP, consumables, cooldowns, status and save/recovery opportunities couple encounters over time.
**Generalized Principle:** Validate encounter chains with incoming-state distributions, not isolated rooms.
**Transfer:** Search worst credible entry state and cumulative resource debt across 3–10 encounter windows.
**Playtest Question:** Which generated sequence creates an unrecoverable downward spiral before the player can make a corrective choice?

### 8. Puzzle solvability is only the floor; solution information and trap topology matter
**Observation:** Two solvable puzzles can differ radically in perceived difficulty and frustration.
**Mechanism:** irreversible moves, misleading locally plausible actions, branching and the amount of information needed to identify a solution shape difficulty.
**Generalized Principle:** Adversarial puzzle search should target low-margin solvability, deceptive branch structure and knowledge-sensitive solution complexity separately.
**Transfer:** Track solution count, shortest solution, irreversible-error depth, dead-end distance, restart cost and policy-conditioned solution information.
**Playtest Question:** Is difficulty coming from insight, or from opaque punishment and expensive recovery?

### 9. Puzzle generators need robustness around the solution, not one solver trace
**Observation:** Guaranteeing one solution can hide brittle content where tiny reasonable deviations lead to unrecoverable failure.
**Mechanism:** a single narrow path through state space produces low strategy robustness.
**Generalized Principle:** Measure the basin of recoverable play around solutions.
**Transfer:** perturb solution traces with locally plausible moves and measure recovery probability/cost; retain puzzles with intended narrowness but flag accidental brittleness.
**Playtest Question:** How many plausible near-solution mistakes remain teachably recoverable?

### 10. Cross-genre metrics are useful as a scaffold, not as release truth
**Observation:** exploration/balance/general playability metrics transfer across domains, but empirical PCG work shows computational metrics can diverge from player perception.
**Mechanism:** generic descriptors omit genre-specific timing, knowledge, motor and recovery costs.
**Generalized Principle:** Use a two-layer objective stack: generic structural invariants plus genre-specific player margins, then human calibration.
**Transfer:** Every automated metric should record the human judgment it is intended to predict and its calibration error.
**Playtest Question:** Which automated metric is currently least predictive of human severity ratings?

### 11. Adversarial objectives should preserve the genre contract while stressing one axis
**Observation:** unconstrained search easily produces content no designer would ship.
**Mechanism:** optimizing failure without style/grammar constraints escapes the intended content manifold.
**Generalized Principle:** Couple every genre objective to sacred genre invariants and bounded semantic mutations.
**Transfer:** Shooter search preserves encounter grammar; racer search preserves lane/course rules; RPG search preserves progression; puzzle search preserves mechanic vocabulary.
**Playtest Question:** Would this failure still be plausible content if the bug were fixed?

### 12. Genre QA needs counterfactual policy pairs
**Observation:** A single agent can confuse content difficulty with agent weakness or exploit strength.
**Mechanism:** different policies reveal whether a generated case tests aiming, routing, planning, resource discipline, execution or an implementation exploit.
**Generalized Principle:** Retain cases where policy outcomes expose a meaningful skill axis; investigate cases where only one idiosyncratic agent fails.
**Transfer:** Compare novice/baseline/expert plus exploit-seeking policies with controlled capability differences.
**Playtest Question:** Does the outcome ordering match the skill model the game claims to reward?

## Production objective matrix

### Shooter
- joint reaction slack
- safe-sector / escape-route availability
- unavoidable-damage lower bound
- pre-encounter ammo/health slack
- spawn occlusion/readability
- pressure-without-recovery duration
- policy disagreement by aim/movement skill

### Racing
- sight-distance minus braking-distance margin
- curvature/steering-rate saturation
- traffic conflict time-to-collision
- overtaking corridor availability
- recovery time after representative contact
- secondary-collision probability
- pit/fuel/recovery reachability where applicable

### RPG / dungeon / encounter
- completion probability distribution
- contribution balance / role redundancy
- strategy dominance
- incoming-state sensitivity
- cumulative resource debt
- recovery/save opportunity distance
- progression/key dependency robustness

### Puzzle
- guaranteed solvability
- shortest/alternate solution structure
- irreversible-error depth
- dead-end/restart cost
- solution-information proxy conditioned on player knowledge
- recoverability after plausible local mistakes
- solver/policy disagreement

## Recommended genre-aware adversarial pipeline

`GENRE CONTRACT → SACRED INVARIANTS → PLAYER-BUDGET DESCRIPTORS → KNOWN-GOOD PARENT → SEMANTIC MUTATION → CHEAP STRUCTURAL GATES → GENRE SIMULATION → POLICY PAIRS → MARGIN VECTOR → HUMAN-CALIBRATED SEVERITY → SHRINK → REGRESSION FIXTURE`

## Release rule

Do not accept “N seeds passed” as procedural release evidence. For each supported genre report:
- structural invalid rate
- genre-specific margin distributions (p1/p5/median)
- severe failure signatures / 1k simulations
- recovery-failure rate
- policy-order inversions
- human-confirmed precision of top adversarial cases
- new boundary cells / compute-hour
- calibration error between machine severity and human severity
