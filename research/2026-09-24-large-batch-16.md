## Observations → mechanisms → principles

### 1. Continue is a pacing decision, not merely monetization
**Observation:** Coin-op continuation can preserve an ongoing run while asking for another credit.
**Mechanism:** Failure cost changes from complete restart to a local resource/payment decision.
**Generalized Principle:** Continue design determines how much mastery, tension and run identity survive failure.
**Transfer:** Short-session games can offer checkpoint continuation, limited revive tokens, or score-sacrificing revive without real-money payment.
**Playtest Question:** After a revive, does the player still care about avoiding the next failure, or has failure become negligible?

### 2. Buy-in must not erase the achievement metric
**Observation:** Additional credits can extend attempts or alter payout opportunity in some coin-op structures.
**Mechanism:** Money/time/resources can become substitutes for execution.
**Generalized Principle:** Separate completion persistence from mastery measurement when buy-in exists.
**Transfer:** Continue may preserve stage progress while resetting score multiplier, rank eligibility, or challenge badge.
**Playtest Question:** Can a player obtain the same mastery signal mainly by spending more attempts/resources?

### 3. Redemption converts score into an exchange language
**Observation:** Redemption modes map play performance to tickets/prizes rather than leaving score purely symbolic.
**Mechanism:** Immediate performance becomes a delayed external reward.
**Generalized Principle:** When score becomes currency, scoring exploits become economy exploits.
**Transfer:** If a modern game turns score into unlock currency, score balance requires economy-grade abuse testing.
**Playtest Question:** Which low-skill repeatable action yields the highest reward per minute?

### 4. Score mode and reward mode may need different rules
**Observation:** Some machines expose materially different score-play and redemption-play configurations.
**Mechanism:** The objective function changes, so identical controls/timers can produce undesirable incentives.
**Generalized Principle:** Do not assume one ruleset optimizes both expressive mastery and reward throughput.
**Transfer:** Separate arcade score attack, progression farming, daily challenge and ranked modes where incentives conflict.
**Playtest Question:** Does the optimal reward-farming strategy still resemble the game's intended skillful play?

### 5. Operator configurability is live design
**Observation:** Coin-op history repeatedly exposes configurable pricing, time, difficulty and mode parameters.
**Mechanism:** A location can tune the product to audience and economics without rebuilding the game.
**Generalized Principle:** Operational parameters are part of game design and need safe ranges, presets and telemetry.
**Transfer:** Event builds and web demos should expose bounded session-length/difficulty presets rather than ad-hoc code edits.
**Playtest Question:** Can a non-designer change one operational setting without accidentally destroying onboarding or fairness?

### 6. Kit economics favor reusable interaction foundations
**Observation:** Gaming Alexandria's Play Meter-based synthesis shows conversion kits overtaking dedicated cabinet purchases around 1989–90 and remaining important afterward.
**Mechanism:** Reusing installed hardware lowers replacement friction and rewards software compatible with known controls/display constraints.
**Generalized Principle:** A reusable platform/core can increase content velocity, but novelty then must come from rules and situations rather than incompatible infrastructure.
**Transfer:** game-core projects should share input/PWA/save/replay primitives while keeping game-specific decision loops distinct.
**Playtest Question:** Is a sequel/update genuinely creating new decisions, or only reskinning the reusable shell?

### 7. Dedicated hardware must earn its footprint
**Observation:** Dedicated cabinets coexist with cheaper conversion kits, while large simulator-style machines can temporarily reverse purchase patterns.
**Mechanism:** Higher physical cost is justified only when the experience visibly cannot be substituted by generic hardware.
**Generalized Principle:** Expensive presentation/input should create a legible experiential advantage.
**Transfer:** Heavy 3D, bespoke haptics, or elaborate UI on mobile should be retained only if they change perception or decisions.
**Playtest Question:** If this expensive feature is disabled, which player decision or sensation disappears?

### 8. Public play has a conversion funnel
**Observation:** Arcade machines operate in spaces where non-players can observe before committing.
**Mechanism:** Attract readability and spectator comprehension reduce uncertainty before first play.
**Generalized Principle:** Treat watch → understand → imagine self playing → start as an onboarding funnel.
**Transfer:** Web landing screens should show one readable interaction and offer one-tap play, not only trailers or menus.
**Playtest Question:** Can a non-player explain the immediate goal after watching 10–20 seconds?

### 9. Linked play creates social information, not just more seats
**Observation:** Networked/multi-cabinet play lets several participants share a competitive/cooperative event while retaining individual stations.
**Mechanism:** Players react to human intent and public state in addition to system state.
**Generalized Principle:** Multiplayer value comes from decisions changed by other people, not player-count multiplication.
**Transfer:** Racing and combat games should expose rival intent, position change and team contribution clearly.
**Playtest Question:** If other humans were replaced by silent ghosts, which meaningful decisions would disappear?

### 10. Team public play needs individual accountability without blame
**Observation:** Public team play benefits from visible contribution, but harsh attribution can damage willingness to replay together.
**Mechanism:** Feedback shapes post-match conversation and social safety.
**Generalized Principle:** Surface contribution in ways that create discussion and role recognition before fault assignment.
**Transfer:** Results can show saves, assists, pressure, recovery and setup rather than only kills/wins.
**Playtest Question:** Does the result screen make teammates want a rematch together?

### 11. Throughput and satisfaction must be measured together
**Observation:** Coin-op operation necessarily couples session duration, turnover and player willingness to continue.
**Mechanism:** Optimizing only throughput can shorten play until perceived value collapses; optimizing only duration can reduce access/rotation.
**Generalized Principle:** Session economics is a two-sided constraint: experience value per attempt and system throughput.
**Transfer:** Convention demos/Playables should measure completion, replay and queue/session length together.
**Playtest Question:** Does shortening the session increase starts while reducing voluntary immediate replays?

### 12. Economy transitions can change what “good play” means
**Observation:** Moving from pure score competition toward ticket/reward structures changes the practical purpose of play.
**Mechanism:** Players optimize whatever is exchangeable or persistent.
**Generalized Principle:** Every persistent reward rewrites the effective victory condition unless carefully aligned.
**Transfer:** XP, currency and battle-pass progress should reward the behavior the core game wants players to learn.
**Playtest Question:** If all explicit rewards vanished, would players still choose roughly the same actions?

## Promotions

Cross-source principles strong enough to promote:
- Operational settings are part of game design; constrain and test them.
- Persistent/exchangeable rewards redefine the effective objective.
- Completion assistance and mastery measurement should be separable.
- Public-play onboarding is a watch-to-play conversion funnel.
- Multiplayer should be tested by whether another person's intent changes decisions.

## Implementation checklist

For a short-session build record: session_seconds, first_failure_seconds, continue_offer_seen, continue_used, completion, immediate_replay, score/rank eligibility, reward_per_minute, spectator_to_start where measurable.

For any reward-linked score system, run exploit probes against idle/safe loops, repetitive low-risk loops, continue farming, intentional death/restart, and time-extension loops.
