## New observations and abstractions

### 1. Visibility is a time series, not a screenshot property
**Observation:** A moving bus, door, crowd, elevator, enemy, or camera-relative foreground prop can hide a cue only during the exact decision window even though canonical screenshots look valid.
**Mechanism:** transient occlusion overlaps the player's commitment deadline.
**Generalized Principle:** validate critical cues over trajectories and time, not only static viewpoints.
**Transfer:** record cue-visible fraction, longest continuous hidden interval, and visibility immediately before commitment.
**Playtest Question:** did a moving object hide the information precisely when the player needed to choose?

### 2. Dynamic occluders and dynamic occludees are different QA roles
**Observation:** rendering systems may support moving objects being culled without treating those same objects as baked occluders.
**Mechanism:** offline visibility structures and runtime depth/query systems have different assumptions.
**Generalized Principle:** QA must classify each dynamic object by whether it may hide gameplay information, be hidden, or both; engine flags are not the design contract.
**Transfer:** maintain semantic `canOccludeCriticalCue` and `mayBeOccluded` tags independent of renderer configuration.
**Playtest Question:** does the test model the moving object's gameplay occlusion even when the engine's baked culling model does not?

### 3. Occlusion latency can create false pop-in failures
**Observation:** query-based dynamic occlusion may return visibility after a frame delay, especially during rapid camera motion.
**Mechanism:** asynchronous visibility feedback trails the camera.
**Generalized Principle:** distinguish geometry/design occlusion failure from renderer-query latency.
**Transfer:** log camera angular velocity with visibility transitions and flag high-speed one-frame pop separately from sustained cue loss.
**Playtest Question:** is the landmark actually badly placed, or is a delayed query causing the apparent disappearance?

### 4. Indoor navigation needs decision-local landmarks
**Observation:** distant/global landmarks can aid initial orientation, while local landmarks become more useful as players learn or traverse enclosed spaces.
**Mechanism:** walls/floors break global line-of-sight and route decisions depend on nearby discriminators.
**Generalized Principle:** multi-floor/interior layouts need a hierarchy of global, floor, zone, and junction landmarks.
**Transfer:** assign each landmark a navigation scope and verify every consequential junction has an appropriate cue before commitment.
**Playtest Question:** when the global landmark disappears indoors, what cue takes over?

### 5. More raw visibility does not guarantee better wayfinding
**Observation:** increasing overall visual access in an indoor environment can add information without proportionally improving navigation.
**Mechanism:** irrelevant visibility competes with task-relevant cues and can create false affordance.
**Generalized Principle:** optimize discriminative visibility, not maximum visibility.
**Transfer:** measure whether visible cues reduce branch ambiguity rather than merely increasing visible-object count.
**Playtest Question:** can the player choose the route faster, or can they merely see more things?

### 6. Floor identity must survive vertical transitions
**Observation:** stairs, lifts, ramps, shafts, teleporters and stacked corridors can produce spatial aliasing when adjacent floors reuse silhouettes and landmarks.
**Mechanism:** vertical movement changes topological context faster than the player rebuilds orientation.
**Generalized Principle:** every vertical transition should provide a before/after orientation bridge.
**Transfer:** test floor-ID cues, arrival-facing direction, visible anchor continuity, and immediate route choices at both ends.
**Playtest Question:** after a vertical transition, can a player state which floor/zone they are on and which way they face without opening a map?

### 7. Door state changes the visibility graph
**Observation:** an open door, closed door, destroyed wall, shutter, lift gate or scripted blocker can transform both traversability and line-of-sight.
**Mechanism:** topology and visibility mutate together.
**Generalized Principle:** stateful portals belong in both navigation-graph and visibility-graph regression tests.
**Transfer:** enumerate meaningful portal states and rerun reachability, cue visibility and branch-readability checks for each.
**Playtest Question:** does opening a shortcut accidentally erase a landmark cue or expose a misleading destination?

### 8. Lighting robustness is semantic, not photometric
**Observation:** a landmark can remain geometrically visible at night yet become functionally unreadable because its contrast, silhouette or identifying feature collapses.
**Mechanism:** geometry visibility and perceptual identification diverge under lighting/exposure/weather changes.
**Generalized Principle:** critical-cue QA needs an identification margin in addition to a line-of-sight result.
**Transfer:** test day/dusk/night/weather/exposure states using screen-space size, local contrast, silhouette separation and human recognition.
**Playtest Question:** can players identify the cue, not merely prove that its pixels are present?

### 9. Lighting transitions need hysteresis around decision points
**Observation:** auto-exposure, entering tunnels, headlights, lightning, bloom or day/night transitions can temporarily wash out navigation cues.
**Mechanism:** adaptation/transient rendering effects overlap a short decision window.
**Generalized Principle:** avoid placing mandatory visual decisions inside unstable adaptation windows unless another modality carries the cue.
**Transfer:** measure cue-recognition margin before/during/after exposure transitions and require a stable interval before commitment.
**Playtest Question:** is the player asked to choose while the image is still adapting?

### 10. Moving occluders require occupancy distributions, not one scripted path
**Observation:** traffic/crowd procedural variation can make a cue readable in one run and blocked in another.
**Mechanism:** stochastic occupancy intersects a narrow sight corridor.
**Generalized Principle:** visibility reliability should be evaluated over occluder distributions.
**Transfer:** replay canonical route with many traffic/crowd seeds; report p5 cue visibility and probability of deadline occlusion.
**Playtest Question:** what fraction of valid seeds hide the cue during the required window?

### 11. Portal visibility should be conservative near boundaries
**Observation:** coarse visibility cells/portals can either over-render or incorrectly hide content near small openings and thin walls.
**Mechanism:** simplified occlusion representation loses geometric detail.
**Generalized Principle:** near-boundary cells and small apertures are adversarial fixtures, not ordinary samples.
**Transfer:** retain worst camera positions near doorframes, corners, stair mouths, windows and atria; compare semantic visibility against ground truth rays/depth.
**Playtest Question:** does a tiny camera move make a critical object incorrectly pop or vanish?

### 12. Navigation QA should test cue handoff, not isolated landmarks
**Observation:** long routes often rely on a sequence: skyline/global anchor → district cue → local landmark → door/sign → objective.
**Mechanism:** each cue has a useful spatial range; failure occurs in gaps between them.
**Generalized Principle:** navigation readability is a relay of overlapping cue envelopes.
**Transfer:** compute cue-coverage intervals along canonical routes and flag uncovered decision-relevant gaps.
**Playtest Question:** when one cue leaves view, is the next cue already understandable?

### 13. Semantic occlusion severity depends on the occluder
**Observation:** temporary blockage by a predictable train or door communicates differently from blockage by arbitrary facade clutter.
**Mechanism:** players can model some occluders and wait/reposition intentionally.
**Generalized Principle:** score occlusion by predictability, duration, agency and consequence, not duration alone.
**Transfer:** classify occluders as periodic, player-controlled, stateful, stochastic or static and weight failures accordingly.
**Playtest Question:** can the player understand why the cue vanished and do something sensible about it?

### 14. Multi-condition regression needs a Cartesian matrix, then adversarial pruning
**Observation:** camera × route × portal state × traffic seed × time-of-day × weather × device can explode combinatorially.
**Mechanism:** exhaustive visual testing becomes too expensive.
**Generalized Principle:** use cheap semantic predicates to search the matrix, then render only worst-N and boundary cases.
**Transfer:** graph/ray/contrast proxies run broadly; full render/human review is reserved for minima and newly discovered clusters.
**Playtest Question:** are expensive captures focused on likely failures rather than uniformly sampling easy states?

## Promoted implementation model

For each critical navigation cue store a contract:

`cueId, scope, routeSegments, decisionId, revealStart, requiredStableWindow, minScreenArea, minIdentificationMargin, allowedOccluderClasses, portalStateMask, lightingProfileMask`

Run each route as a temporal trace. For every decision compute:

- cue handoff gap
- stable-visible duration before commitment
- p5 visibility across dynamic-occluder seeds
- longest hidden interval
- visibility-transition/chatter rate
- semantic occluder class
- identification margin by lighting profile
- floor/zone disambiguation score

Escalate the worst cases to rendered captures and human recognition tests.

## QA fixtures added conceptually

- moving truck/bus crosses landmark sightline just before junction
- crowd density worst-case around an exit sign
- rapid camera yaw while hardware occlusion result lags
- door open/closed/destroyed variants
- elevator/stairs arrival on visually similar floors
- atrium and stacked corridor with cross-floor false destination
- tunnel entry/exit with exposure transition
- day/dusk/night/weather variants
- small doorway / thin-wall / corner camera boundary
- global-to-local landmark handoff gap
- stochastic traffic seeds that maximize deadline occlusion
- lowest-end shipped camera/FOV/device aspect profile
