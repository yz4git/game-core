## Observations → Mechanisms → Generalized Principles → Transfer → Playtest Questions

### 1. A highlight is a change in meaning, not merely a large event
**Observation:** Event-triggered systems naturally find kills/scores, while research adds novelty, audiovisual and social/reaction signals.
**Mechanism:** Spectator value often comes from a state transition: lead reversal, escape, setup payoff, unexpected solution, near failure, or social reaction.
**Generalized Principle:** Rank highlights by narrative/state delta, not raw event magnitude alone.
**Transfer:** Combine objective swing, survival probability change, rarity, setup duration and reaction signals.
**Playtest Question:** Do top-ranked clips remain interesting when obvious kills/scores are removed?

### 2. Highlight quality needs negative examples
**Observation:** Long gameplay contains many visually active but semantically routine intervals.
**Mechanism:** A detector trained only on positive exciting events learns motion/noise instead of importance.
**Generalized Principle:** Evaluate highlight models against hard negatives that look exciting but change little.
**Transfer:** Build corpora containing routine firefights, repeated combo finishers, cosmetic explosions and low-stakes overtakes.
**Playtest Question:** How often does the detector select visually loud but strategically irrelevant moments?

### 3. Multi-modal agreement increases confidence, disagreement carries information
**Observation:** Research combines video, audio, player/streamer reactions and other streams.
**Mechanism:** Independent cues can confirm significance; disagreement can identify subtle strategic or socially funny moments.
**Generalized Principle:** Preserve per-modality scores before fusion rather than collapsing immediately to one excitement number.
**Transfer:** Store gameplay-event, audio, camera-motion, social/reaction and novelty channels separately.
**Playtest Question:** Which valuable clips are discovered only because modalities disagree?

### 4. Personalized highlights and canonical highlights are different products
**Observation:** A player's memorable moment can differ from the audience's most legible moment.
**Mechanism:** Personal context includes prior failures, goals and relationships invisible to generic event ranking.
**Generalized Principle:** Separate globally understandable highlights from player-specific memories.
**Transfer:** Canonical feed favors legibility/state swing; personal feed can weight achievements, rivals, PBs and repeated attempts.
**Playtest Question:** Does one ranking satisfy both strangers and the player who created the replay?

### 5. Clip boundaries matter as much as event detection
**Observation:** Detecting an exciting instant does not automatically include the setup needed to understand it.
**Mechanism:** Too-short clips remove causality; too-long clips dilute payoff.
**Generalized Principle:** Highlight extraction should locate setup → tension → resolution, not just a peak timestamp.
**Transfer:** Backtrack to possession/encounter/decision onset and extend through outcome stabilization.
**Playtest Question:** Can a first-time viewer explain why the highlighted outcome was difficult or surprising?

### 6. Highlight diversity needs an explicit budget
**Observation:** Ranking by one score repeatedly selects the same event class.
**Mechanism:** Top-k optimization collapses toward frequent/high-amplitude patterns.
**Generalized Principle:** A highlight reel should optimize coverage across meaningful event types, actors and pacing.
**Transfer:** Penalize duplicate event signatures and cap consecutive clips from one player/type.
**Playtest Question:** Does a five-clip reel communicate more than one reason the match/run was interesting?

### 7. Human editorial choices are calibration data, not absolute truth
**Observation:** Research can learn from edited clips or human labels, but editors optimize for audience/platform goals.
**Mechanism:** Labels encode editorial policy as well as intrinsic gameplay importance.
**Generalized Principle:** Treat human labels as target-context evidence and document the audience/purpose behind them.
**Transfer:** Maintain separate validation sets for coaching, entertainment, personal memory and competitive recap.
**Playtest Question:** Does a model calibrated on social clips still choose useful coaching moments?

### 8. Spectator delay does not protect secrets already present in the spectator payload
**Observation:** Delaying a shared state stream changes timing but not which fields the observer receives.
**Mechanism:** A delayed secret is still disclosed; collusion only becomes slower.
**Generalized Principle:** Hidden information must be removed or transformed at the authorization boundary before spectator replication.
**Transfer:** Build spectator-specific schemas/views rather than delaying the full player state.
**Playtest Question:** Can any spectator packet reveal fog-of-war entities, private inventory, RNG future state or team-only markers?

### 9. Information safety should be field-level, not screen-level
**Observation:** Replay/recording data can contain identifiers or debug/private state not visible in normal UI.
**Mechanism:** Exporting internal state bypasses visual privacy assumptions.
**Generalized Principle:** Classify replay fields by visibility and sharing scope independently of rendering.
**Transfer:** PUBLIC / PARTICIPANT / TEAM / PRIVATE / DEBUG field labels with export allowlists.
**Playtest Question:** Does public export contain anything the public renderer never shows?

### 10. Delay is a competitive policy with a threat model
**Observation:** Spectator delay is commonly treated as a scalar setting, but games differ in how long information remains actionable.
**Mechanism:** The exploit window depends on travel time, decision horizon, communication latency and match structure.
**Generalized Principle:** Choose delay from the lifetime of actionable hidden information, not a conventional round number.
**Transfer:** Estimate secret-value half-life per mode and test collusion scenarios.
**Playtest Question:** After the configured delay, can leaked information still alter a live competitive decision?

### 11. Replay controls are an automation surface
**Observation:** Riot's Replay API exposes playback, seek, render, recording and keyframed sequences.
**Mechanism:** Machine-addressable timeline/camera controls allow candidate events to become reproducible clips without manual capture.
**Generalized Principle:** Design replay UX primitives so automated QA/highlight tooling can invoke the same operations.
**Transfer:** Stable seek-by-tick, follow-entity, camera preset, overlay toggle and deterministic render endpoints.
**Playtest Question:** Can a detected event be rendered into a consistent clip without human camera correction?

### 12. Highlight evaluation should include comprehension, not only engagement
**Observation:** Excitement/reaction signals detect attention but cannot guarantee causal understanding.
**Mechanism:** A confusing spectacle can generate strong reaction while teaching nothing about the game.
**Generalized Principle:** Evaluate automated clips on interest and comprehension separately.
**Transfer:** Ask viewers what changed, why it mattered, and who caused it; measure answer accuracy alongside watch/share rate.
**Playtest Question:** Which highly engaging clips fail the causal-understanding test?

### 13. Privacy-preserving telemetry can still train ranking
**Observation:** Highlight systems benefit from behavior/reaction data, while replay systems may contain sensitive identity/content.
**Mechanism:** Many ranking features need aggregate timing or event counts, not raw chat, face, voice or identifiers.
**Generalized Principle:** Collect the lowest-information signal that answers the ranking question.
**Transfer:** Prefer aggregate rewind/event-jump rates and anonymous clip ratings; make richer social/biometric signals opt-in where appropriate.
**Playtest Question:** Can the same ranking decision be made after deleting raw identity/content fields?

### 14. Highlight generation can become a game-design diagnostic
**Observation:** If an hour of play yields only one repeated highlight signature, the detector may be revealing experiential monotony rather than merely a media problem.
**Mechanism:** Distribution of meaningful state changes approximates where memorable decisions occur.
**Generalized Principle:** Use highlight-signature diversity as a secondary pacing/content-health metric.
**Transfer:** CI/playtest reports can chart meaningful-event density and duplicate-signature ratio by level/seed.
**Playtest Question:** Are long low-highlight regions intentional recovery/exploration, or evidence that meaningful decisions have collapsed?

## Promotions

Strong cross-source principles:
- Highlights should optimize meaningful state change plus comprehension, not spectacle alone.
- Automated highlight QA requires hard negatives and diversity constraints.
- Spectator delay is not an authorization mechanism; hidden fields must be excluded before replication.
- Replay/export privacy must be field-level and allowlist-based.
- Replay control primitives should be automation-addressable for QA and clip generation.
- Highlight-signature distribution can diagnose pacing/content repetition.

## Implementation / QA metrics

For each candidate clip record only what is needed:
- event type/signature
- state-delta score
- rarity/novelty
- setup duration
- resolution duration
- modality confidence vector
- duplicate distance to already-selected clips
- comprehension-test score
- human/editor rating where available

Spectator security tests:
- schema diff PLAYER vs SPECTATOR
- forbidden-field assertion
- collusion simulation at configured delay
- public-export allowlist scan
- participant/team/private/debug field leak tests

Privacy rule: do not store raw chat, voice, face, account identifiers or other sensitive content merely because a highlight model could consume it; justify each richer signal separately.
