# A. Rhythm / music games

## A1. Timing strictness is emotional design, not only scoring precision

### Observation
PaRappa developer Masaya Matsuura described early builds as much more forgiving. They were easy to clear, but lacked the tension and live-performance feeling he wanted; the final timing was deliberately stricter.

### Mechanism
A timing window controls not only success rate but the player's bodily state: loose timing feels permissive; tight timing creates concentration and performance pressure.

### Generalized Principle
**Choose timing windows from the intended performance emotion, then tune accessibility around that target.**

### Transfer
- strict score layer + wider survival layer
- novice mode can preserve the beat while relaxing failure
- high-rank windows can remain narrow without making song completion equally narrow

### Playtest Question
Do players describe the same chart as “musical tension” or merely “the game ignored me”?

---

## A2. The visual marker must agree with the musical event

### Observation
Early DDR material records a notorious case where a fast song's note timing was intentionally shifted because staff expected players could not keep up; the offset instead made the song harder to play and was corrected later.

### Mechanism
Rhythm players form an internal prediction from the music. If the scoring target contradicts the audible beat, skill becomes compensation for the software rather than musicianship.

### Principle
**In rhythm play, audiovisual disagreement is a rules bug, not cosmetic polish.**

### Transfer
Web/iPhone rhythm games should test AudioContext scheduling, rendering, touch timestamps and output latency separately.

### Playtest Question
Can a musically trained tester play accurately by ear without learning an artificial offset?

---

## A3. Immediate note feedback closes the timing-learning loop

### Observation
Early DDR feedback was harder to read when successfully hit arrows continued moving instead of clearly disappearing; later revision improved this.

### Mechanism
For millisecond-scale skills, the player needs immediate confirmation of which input was judged and how.

### Principle
**Timing feedback must identify the judged event, not only show a global score change.**

### Transfer
On hit:
- consume/highlight the exact note
- show early/late where useful
- give distinct audio/haptic confirmation

### Playtest Question
After one miss, can the player tell whether they were early, late, wrong-input, or never registered?

---

## A4. Beginner protection can preserve a complete musical phrase

### Observation
DDR's early EASY mode included instruction and could let a novice finish a song rather than terminating immediately on failure.

### Mechanism
Stopping a song early destroys the very musical continuity the genre is teaching.

### Principle
**For novice rhythm learning, preserve phrase/song continuity before enforcing harsh fail states.**

### Transfer
Practice mode can record failure but continue playback, then summarize weak measures afterward.

### Playtest Question
Does a beginner get enough uninterrupted music to discover the beat before the game ejects them?

---

## A5. Advanced rhythm play should permit expression beyond imitation

### Observation
PaRappa's COOL scoring rewarded personal arrangement rather than only exact reproduction.

### Mechanism
Once the base rhythm is understood, allowing controlled deviation changes mastery from copying into interpretation.

### Principle
**After teaching synchronization, create an expert layer where musically valid variation can score.**

### Transfer
- optional fills
- freestyle windows
- syncopation bonuses
- phrase-level variation judged against musical structure

### Playtest Question
Can experts demonstrate musical understanding in more than one exact input sequence?

# B. 3D camera and targeting

## B1. Lock-on solves a coordinate problem before it solves a combat problem

### Observation
Ocarina of Time's developers traced Z-targeting to difficulty aligning characters and facing targets in 3D; even interacting with signs exposed axis problems.

### Mechanism
Free 3D movement creates separate player, camera and target axes. Lock-on temporarily collapses them into a shared relational frame.

### Principle
**Use targeting to reduce coordinate ambiguity when precision matters; do not treat lock-on merely as aim assist.**

### Transfer
Melee, interaction, dialogue and contextual actions can all benefit from temporary relational framing.

### Playtest Question
When an action misses, did the player misunderstand distance/timing, or did camera and character axes disagree?

---

## B2. Camera and enemy scheduling are one combat system

### Observation
The Ocarina team observed staged sword fights where surrounding enemies do not all attack simultaneously. Z-targeting helped formalize one active relationship while other enemies waited.

### Mechanism
A camera can only clearly frame a limited number of simultaneous high-priority threats.

### Principle
**Budget simultaneous attackers against what the camera can communicate, not against raw enemy count.**

### Transfer
In 3D crowd combat, separate:
- visible pressure enemies
- active attacker
- incoming next attacker
- offscreen repositioners

### Playtest Question
How many independently timed attacks can the player actually read in the current framing?

---

## B3. A target marker should communicate relationship, not merely selection

### Observation
The Ocarina prototype used a simple target marker, then transformed that functional marker into Navi, integrating navigation feedback with the world.

### Mechanism
Selection state is critical combat information. Diegetic or characterful representation can preserve readability without feeling like debug UI.

### Principle
**Critical targeting information may be stylized, but its state change must remain unambiguous.**

### Playtest Question
Can players instantly tell target acquired, target lost and target switched without reading text?

---

## B4. Camera autonomy and manual override should coexist

### Observation
Super Mario 64's manual exposed Lakitu camera distance, Mario-Cam, and a way to halt camera movement rather than forcing one universal behavior.

### Mechanism
Automatic framing reduces workload most of the time, but unusual geometry creates cases where player intent differs from the heuristic.

### Principle
**Automate the common camera case and provide a low-friction override for exceptions.**

### Playtest Question
How often does the player fight the camera rather than use the override intentionally?

# C. Inventory as gameplay

## C1. Capacity is interesting when packing changes the answer

### Observation
Resident Evil 4's attaché case uses a finite grid where items occupy different footprints; capacity upgrades expand the grid.

### Mechanism
The constraint is spatial rather than a single weight number. Rearrangement can recover usable capacity, so organization itself becomes a small optimization problem.

### Principle
**If inventory limits are meant to be gameplay, let organization or loadout composition change what fits.**

### Transfer
Avoid making a carry limit only a repeated “inventory full” interruption.

### Playtest Question
When full, does the player make a meaningful loadout decision or simply perform housekeeping?

---

## C2. Capacity upgrades can be convenience progression without removing scarcity

### Observation
RE4 expands the case from the starting grid to larger purchasable cases, but even the largest remains finite.

### Mechanism
Progression reduces friction while preserving the underlying choice.

### Principle
**Upgrade the player's decision space before eliminating the constraint entirely.**

### Playtest Question
Does the late-game inventory still create occasional tradeoffs without recreating early-game clutter?

# D. Procedural generation

## D1. Generate for the intended verb, not for abstract variety

### Observation
Roguelike Radio's gameplay-oriented procedural-generation discussion includes examples where Rogue-style dungeon architecture was rejected because it did not fit the actual game being made.

### Mechanism
A technically valid map can still suppress the movement, combat or exploration behavior the game is supposed to produce.

### Principle
**A generator should be evaluated by whether it creates opportunities for the target mechanics.**

### Transfer
WorldSeed / roguelike stages should score generated spaces for driving, sightlines, encounter roles or navigation—not only connectivity.

### Playtest Question
What intended verb does each generated region invite?

---

## D2. Solvability is only the first quality gate

### Observation
Intentional Computational Level Design explicitly distinguishes playable generated scenes from scenes that create opportunities for targeted mechanics.

### Mechanism
A path from start to goal proves validity, not quality.

### Principle
**Procedural QA needs layered gates: valid → solvable → mechanically useful → paced → distinctive.**

### Playtest Question
What percentage of valid seeds are actually worth shipping without regeneration?

---

## D3. Simple robust actors combine better with random placement

### Observation
Derek Yu's Spelunky discussion emphasizes simple, versatile enemies and interactions so probabilistic placement remains understandable while combinations create surprises.

### Mechanism
Highly scripted enemies depend on authored context; simple rule-driven enemies tolerate more generated contexts.

### Principle
**The more procedural the placement, the more locally robust and legible each component should be.**

### Playtest Question
How many legal placements make this enemy unfair, inert or nonsensical?

---

## D4. Stable seeds turn randomness into a debuggable artifact

### Observation
Classic roguelike generation often creates a dungeon once and preserves it for re-entry; modern procedural workflows similarly depend on reproducible generated states.

### Mechanism
If a bad layout cannot be reproduced, designers cannot diagnose or compare fixes.

### Principle
**Every generated world used in QA should have a reproducible seed and generation-version identifier.**

### Transfer
Store seed + generator version + difficulty + biome in bug reports.

### Playtest Question
Can a tester reproduce the exact bad generation after the generator code changes?

---

## D5. Surprise should emerge from interactions, not arbitrary rule changes

### Observation
Rogue randomized item identities while keeping the underlying item system learnable; Spelunky combines stable component rules in variable layouts.

### Mechanism
Players can be surprised while still applying learned causal models.

### Principle
**Randomize arrangement and identity mappings more readily than fundamental physics or response rules.**

### Playtest Question
After a surprising death, can the player explain it using rules they already knew?

---

# E. Local multiplayer / shared information

## E1. Shared-screen multiplayer has a social information channel

### Observation
Historical local multiplayer puts opponents or partners in the same physical space, where reactions, speech and shared screen state become part of play.

### Mechanism
Players can coordinate, bluff, teach and celebrate without explicit in-game communication systems.

### Principle
**Local multiplayer should exploit shared attention rather than imitate online multiplayer with fewer screens.**

### Transfer
- readable common objectives
- short verbal callouts
- visible role dependencies
- rapid rematch

### Playtest Question
Does being physically together create useful decisions or only logistical inconvenience?

# Cross-domain synthesis

## 1. Calibration is part of the rules

Rhythm timing, camera framing, targeting alignment and procedural constraints all reveal the same pattern: a game can have correct abstract rules but feel wrong if the presentation/input coordinate system is miscalibrated.

**Design rule:** Test the transformation between player intention and judged game state, not only the state machine itself.

## 2. Constraint quality depends on agency inside the constraint

A tight rhythm window, finite inventory, lock-on target, or procedural rule can all create depth when the player has meaningful choices inside the restriction. The same restriction becomes friction when there is only one tedious response.

## 3. Generation requires a quality floor, not only variety

More possible worlds are not automatically better. A smaller set of consistently meaningful worlds can outperform a vast space containing dull or unfair seeds.

## 4. Expert expression should grow after basic synchronization

PaRappa's arrangement layer, camera override, inventory packing and procedural route exploitation share a structure:
- novice follows the obvious solution
- expert bends the system without breaking its rules

This is a reusable mastery pattern.

## Recommended promotions

These findings are sufficiently cross-domain to promote in a later core-principle merge:
- calibration/presentation agreement is part of game rules
- preserve musical/interaction continuity in beginner modes
- camera and encounter scheduling must be tuned together
- procedural QA requires a quality floor beyond solvability
- generated QA states must be reproducible
- constraints should create decisions, not housekeeping
