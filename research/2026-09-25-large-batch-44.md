Date: 2026-09-25

## New Observation → Mechanism → Generalized Principle → Transfer → Playtest Question

### 1. Single-boundary coverage misses interaction failures
Observation: two individually safe margins can jointly become unsafe.
Mechanism: player response consumes several budgets at once.
Generalized Principle: cover pairs of decision-relevant boundaries before escalating selectively to higher-order combinations.
Transfer: speed × sight distance; enemy density × escape width; ammo × recovery time.
Playtest Question: which passing single margins fail when both approach their limits?

### 2. Boundary pairs need semantic buckets
Observation: many numeric parameter pairs do not alter player decisions.
Mechanism: implementation variables are often redundant proxies.
Generalized Principle: define pair coverage over player-budget/margin families.
Transfer: reaction, clearance, resource, information, recovery and precision.
Playtest Question: does the pair represent two distinct player constraints?

### 3. Pair coverage is a floor, not completeness
Observation: interaction-fault research shows important faults can require three or more factors.
Mechanism: some failures emerge only when a third condition activates the pair.
Generalized Principle: start with pairwise boundary coverage, then promote suspicious families to targeted t-way tests.
Transfer: rain × night × speed; low HP × status × encounter topology.
Playtest Question: which pair remains benign until a third condition is present?

### 4. Promote pairs using evidence
Observation: exhaustive t-way growth is too expensive.
Mechanism: severe failures, disagreement, near-zero margins and human confirmation identify risky neighborhoods.
Generalized Principle: increase interaction strength selectively.
Transfer: pair to triple only for high-risk families.
Playtest Question: what evidence justifies spending triple-wise budget here?

### 5. Repair distance is part of the result
Observation: two repaired candidates can both be valid but require radically different correction.
Mechanism: large repair deltas may erase the intended adversarial mutation.
Generalized Principle: validity after repair is insufficient; record how far repair moved the candidate.
Transfer: roads, rooms, encounters, puzzles and spawn layouts.
Playtest Question: did repair preserve the failure hypothesis or replace the case?

### 6. Repair distance must be semantic and normalized
Observation: raw edit counts cannot compare moving one boss with changing many decorative props.
Mechanism: edits differ in gameplay consequence and scale.
Generalized Principle: measure repair in normalized semantic dimensions, not only nodes changed.
Transfer: topology, clearance, resource, timing and visibility deltas.
Playtest Question: which gameplay-relevant dimensions did repair alter?

### 7. Separate invariant repair from hypothesis repair
Observation: a repair may restore legality while also fixing the boundary being tested.
Mechanism: projection to feasibility can cross the target failure boundary.
Generalized Principle: classify repair components as invariant-restoring versus hypothesis-altering.
Transfer: connectivity restoration versus widening the tested choke.
Playtest Question: did repair touch the margin under investigation?

### 8. Calibrate repair thresholds from downstream fidelity
Observation: a universal repair-distance cutoff is arbitrary.
Mechanism: acceptable repair depends on whether behavior and failure identity survive.
Generalized Principle: fit thresholds against behavior retention, failure retention and human equivalence.
Transfer: historical candidate-to-repair pairs become calibration fixtures.
Playtest Question: at what repair distance does behavior identity collapse?

### 9. Use matched boundary pairs for causal diagnosis
Observation: a failing case alone does not reveal which interaction mattered.
Mechanism: matched neighbors differing in one margin isolate interaction effects.
Generalized Principle: archive pass/fail boundary pairs, not only failures.
Transfer: same seed with speed or clearance changed by a small delta.
Playtest Question: can one small counterfactual flip the outcome?

### 10. Weight reachable and consequential pairs
Observation: some theoretical boundary pairs are unreachable or harmless.
Mechanism: generator constraints and game rules eliminate combinations.
Generalized Principle: report raw pair coverage beside reachable/consequence-weighted coverage.
Transfer: classify impossible, unknown, observed-safe, near-boundary and confirmed-failure.
Playtest Question: are uncovered pairs actually reachable and decision-relevant?

### 11. Repair cost can reveal generator brittleness
Observation: repeated large repairs in one region mean generation and validity constraints are misaligned.
Mechanism: mutation routinely lands far outside the feasible manifold.
Generalized Principle: aggregate repair distance by region/operator as a generator-health metric.
Transfer: tune operators or generator grammar, not just QA.
Playtest Question: which regions consume disproportionate repair work?

### 12. Shrinking must preserve interaction identity
Observation: generic shrinking can remove one member of the triggering pair.
Mechanism: minimal size is not the same as minimal causal interaction.
Generalized Principle: shrink while preserving the boundary-pair signature and validity.
Transfer: retain pair labels through semantic shrink.
Playtest Question: does the minimized fixture still require the same two margins?

## Production model
For each candidate retain: boundary families, normalized margins, mutation delta, invariant-repair delta, hypothesis-repair delta, repair-distance vector, outcome/signature, matched counterfactual and human label.

Suggested pair-state labels: UNREACHABLE, UNKNOWN, OBSERVED_INTERIOR, NEAR_BOUNDARY_PASS, NEAR_BOUNDARY_FAIL, HUMAN_CONFIRMED, PROMOTE_TO_T3.

Do not use one global pair-coverage target. Define a mandatory set from genre contracts and observed severe families, then track reachable/consequence-weighted coverage plus holdout discoveries.

## Implementation / playtest use
1. Define 6–12 player-budget margin families per generator.
2. Generate the semantically valid pair matrix.
3. Remove only proven-impossible pairs; keep unknown separate.
4. Require matched near-boundary pass/fail samples for high-risk pairs.
5. Record repair distance as a vector and whether repair touched the hypothesis.
6. Promote severe/disagreement pairs to targeted 3-way tests.
7. Calibrate repair thresholds from retained behavior/signature.
8. Turn human-confirmed pairs into permanent regression fixtures.
