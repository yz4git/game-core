## Observation → Mechanism → Generalized Principle → Transfer → Playtest Question

### 1. Reachability is a probability field, not a fixed rectangle
**Observation:** Smartphone thumb performance changes with device/grip span and target position; larger grip span can reduce hit performance and thumb-tip movement.
**Mechanism:** Joint range, grip stabilization and reach direction create unequal motor cost across the screen.
**Generalized Principle:** Treat touch placement as a reachability field conditioned on device, hand and posture, not a universal left/right template.
**Transfer:** Generate candidate control anchors from safe area + device dimensions + handedness, then validate high-frequency actions against reach cost.
**Playtest Question:** Which 10% of screen locations produce the highest miss/reposition rate for each hand/device class?

### 2. High-frequency actions deserve the cheapest reach
**Observation:** Apple recommends putting frequent virtual controls near the player's thumbs and secondary controls farther away.
**Mechanism:** Repeated long reaches accumulate motor time and fatigue even when each individual reach succeeds.
**Generalized Principle:** Allocate ergonomic budget by action frequency × urgency × precision requirement.
**Transfer:** Attack/dodge/steer receive prime thumb zones; map/settings can tolerate remote placement.
**Playtest Question:** Over 30 minutes, how many high-frequency actions require leaving the natural grip?

### 3. Handedness presets should mirror function, not blindly mirror geometry
**Observation:** Touch layouts can be dynamically positioned while gameplay conventions may assign movement/camera to distinct hands.
**Mechanism:** Pure mirroring can move a control physically but also invert learned role expectations or conflict with HUD/world information.
**Generalized Principle:** A left-handed preset should preserve semantic roles while re-optimizing reach and occlusion.
**Transfer:** Store controls by role (move, camera, primary, secondary), then solve layout constraints per handedness rather than x→width-x.
**Playtest Question:** Does the alternate-hand preset reduce reach cost without increasing role confusion?

### 4. Dynamic controls should follow the hand, but boundedly
**Observation:** Apple recommends placing a virtual movement stick where the player puts their thumb rather than forcing a single fixed origin.
**Mechanism:** Local recentering reduces acquisition distance and accommodates grip variation.
**Generalized Principle:** Dynamic placement should adapt within a predictable capture region and stable neutral model.
**Transfer:** Floating stick appears near touch-down but clamps to safe ergonomic bounds and uses a consistent dead zone.
**Playtest Question:** Does dynamic recentering reduce acquisition time without causing accidental movement or loss of spatial memory?

### 5. Control size and visual size are separate parameters
**Observation:** Apple specifies generous touch target sizes; a control's hit region can be larger than its visible icon.
**Mechanism:** Finger contact area and visual occlusion make pixel-perfect targeting inappropriate.
**Generalized Principle:** Optimize hit geometry for motor confidence while optimizing visual geometry for scene readability.
**Transfer:** Use oversized invisible hit regions, overlap resolution rules and debug visualization of actual targets.
**Playtest Question:** Can miss rate be reduced without increasing HUD clutter?

### 6. Occlusion is an input cost that can be measured
**Observation:** Direct touch and virtual controls necessarily place fingers over display content.
**Mechanism:** The same finger that acts also removes visual information near the action point.
**Generalized Principle:** Measure finger-covered critical information as part of control quality.
**Transfer:** Instrument critical-target-under-thumb time and shift telegraphs/reticles away from persistent thumb zones.
**Playtest Question:** During failure moments, was the relevant threat/target covered by a finger in the preceding 500 ms?

### 7. Touch can collapse multi-button chords into temporal gestures
**Observation:** Apple's touch guidance suggests combining functions and using tap/hold/double-tap variants where appropriate.
**Mechanism:** Temporal variation lets one large reachable region encode related actions without demanding multiple tiny simultaneous buttons.
**Generalized Principle:** Reduce spatial control count by grouping semantically related actions before reducing gameplay decisions.
**Transfer:** tap=attack, hold=charged attack; stick magnitude=walk/run, where timing ambiguity remains acceptable.
**Playtest Question:** Does the merged control preserve intentional choice, or merely create gesture-recognition errors?

### 8. Adaptive visibility is an ergonomic feature, not only visual polish
**Observation:** Virtual controls can fade or hide when irrelevant and become stronger when active.
**Mechanism:** Contextual visibility frees visual space while preserving the same interaction region/role.
**Generalized Principle:** Separate control availability, hit region and visual prominence.
**Transfer:** Inactive stick may fade while its capture zone remains; unavailable action may disappear only if the state change is predictable.
**Playtest Question:** Can players still locate the control without looking directly at it after it fades?

### 9. Haptic density has a fatigue and information budget
**Observation:** Haptic systems can encode transient/continuous patterns with intensity and sharpness; not every event needs tactile output.
**Mechanism:** Frequent low-value vibration competes with meaningful tactile events and can contribute to sensory/device burden.
**Generalized Principle:** Reserve haptics for state changes whose tactile channel improves confirmation or urgency; measure events/minute.
**Transfer:** Prioritize parry, collision, lock/acquire, dangerous grip loss; omit routine decorative pulses or offer intensity/off controls.
**Playtest Question:** After 30 minutes, which haptic events can players still distinguish and name?

### 10. Thermal change can become an ergonomics regression
**Observation:** Mobile play occurs while the player physically grips the compute/display device; long sessions differ from short lab tests.
**Mechanism:** Heat, battery state and sustained rendering can alter grip comfort and performance, causing grip shifts that invalidate a layout tuned cold.
**Generalized Principle:** Evaluate controls under sustained thermal/performance conditions, not only fresh-launch conditions.
**Transfer:** Run 30–60 minute device tests while logging temperature proxy/performance, grip reposition and miss rate.
**Playtest Question:** Does miss rate or grip-shift frequency increase after sustained load even when frame rate remains playable?

### 11. Cross-input equivalence should preserve decision bandwidth
**Observation:** Apple recommends supporting platform-default touch alongside optional physical controllers rather than assuming one input method fits all.
**Mechanism:** Different devices offer different tactile landmarks, simultaneous inputs, pointing directness and precision.
**Generalized Principle:** Equivalent controls need not have identical gestures; they should preserve the same important decisions and response opportunities.
**Transfer:** Compare touch/controller/keyboard using acquisition time, simultaneous-action capability, precision, correction time and fatigue rather than button count.
**Playtest Question:** Does one input method remove a tactical choice or impose an extra attention task absent from the others?

### 12. Input feedback must remain visible outside the finger footprint
**Observation:** Apple's game-control guidance recommends visible pressed states that remain perceivable while the finger covers the control, combined where useful with sound/haptics.
**Mechanism:** A highlight confined under the finger cannot confirm acquisition.
**Generalized Principle:** Confirmation must escape the occluding actuator.
**Transfer:** Use outer glow/ring, nearby direction marker, reticle reaction, sound or haptic confirmation.
**Playtest Question:** Can a player confirm press acquisition without moving the finger or looking away from gameplay?

### 13. Layout adaptation needs stability hysteresis
**Observation:** Flexible/adaptive layouts are useful across device sizes, but controls are also learned spatially.
**Mechanism:** Frequent movement of controls destroys motor memory even when every new location is individually reachable.
**Generalized Principle:** Adapt at session/setup boundaries or meaningful posture changes; avoid continuous layout drift.
**Transfer:** Persist a chosen layout; use hysteresis before switching anchors after orientation/size changes.
**Playtest Question:** How often does a player's learned target location move during normal play?

### 14. Accessibility and ergonomics share the same parameterization layer
**Observation:** Apple emphasizes customizable/accessibility-aware input and alternatives to specific physical movements.
**Mechanism:** Size, position, hold/toggle, sensitivity and feedback parameters that reduce fatigue also remove barriers for varied motor capability.
**Generalized Principle:** Build ergonomics as configurable input semantics rather than a separate accessibility patch.
**Transfer:** Expose handedness, scale, spacing, stick mode, hold/toggle, sensitivity, haptic intensity and remap from one input profile system.
**Playtest Question:** Can a player change comfort parameters without changing the intended game rule or losing competitive clarity?

## Promotions

Cross-source principles suitable for core/playtest promotion:
- Ergonomic cost should be allocated by action frequency × urgency × precision.
- Touch hit geometry and visual geometry should be independently tunable.
- Adaptive controls need predictable bounds and layout stability.
- Cross-input parity means semantic/decision equivalence, not identical button maps.
- Long-session control QA must include fatigue, grip shifts and sustained-device conditions.
- Haptics have an information/fatigue budget.

## Implementation / telemetry

Per device/layout profile, capture where practical:
- hand/preset (only when explicitly selected; do not infer identity)
- viewport/safe-area class
- action target acquisition time
- miss/cancel rate by control
- drag distance and recenter count
- simultaneous-input conflicts
- critical-information-under-thumb time
- grip/layout manual adjustments
- haptic events/minute
- session-duration buckets
- performance/thermal proxy and late-session miss-rate delta

Do not infer handedness from personal identity; treat it as a user-selected control preference or anonymous test condition.
