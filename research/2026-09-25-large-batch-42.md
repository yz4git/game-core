## New Observation → Mechanism → Principle → Transfer → Playtest Question

### 1. Flat discovery is not exhaustion
Observation: difficult regions can create long quiet intervals.
Mechanism: sparse gateways, weak operators and expensive regions delay novelty.
Principle: require repeated saturation evidence, not one plateau.
Transfer: require multiple rolling windows and independent cohorts.
Playtest Question: did the generator saturate, or did the search policy stall?

### 2. Raw occupied-cell count overstates useful coverage
Observation: many cells can be mechanically equivalent or low consequence.
Mechanism: descriptor binning can manufacture numerical novelty.
Principle: weight coverage by semantic distinction and gameplay consequence.
Transfer: report consequence-weighted coverage beside raw occupancy.
Playtest Question: do new cells change player decisions?

### 3. The true reachable denominator is partly unknown
Observation: configured descriptor cells are not all necessarily reachable.
Mechanism: generator constraints, repair and downstream rules remove regions.
Principle: never equate configured-bin percentage with reachable coverage.
Transfer: classify cells as observed, structurally impossible, suspected reachable, or unknown.
Playtest Question: are empty cells impossible, hard to reach, or neglected?

### 4. Thresholds are generator-family specific
Observation: shooter, puzzle, racer, RPG and city generators have different tail rarity and evaluation cost.
Mechanism: their player budgets and consequence surfaces differ.
Principle: there is no universal “N seeds without novelty” rule.
Transfer: fit thresholds per generator/ruleset/descriptor cohort from historical runs.
Playtest Question: is the threshold supported by this generator's history?

### 5. Calibrate with holdout continuation
Observation: a stop rule is only useful if missed discoveries are measured.
Mechanism: without continuing past a candidate stop point, false stops are invisible.
Principle: evaluate stopping rules counterfactually.
Transfer: mark candidate T in historical runs, continue to T+delta, and count actionable discoveries that would have been missed.
Playtest Question: what appeared after the rule would have stopped?

### 6. Consequential discovery matters more than novelty alone
Observation: harmless variety can keep increasing while severe boundaries remain untouched.
Mechanism: raw novelty is weakly coupled to player risk.
Principle: stopping should be risk-oriented.
Transfer: keep separate curves for raw cells, boundary cells, severe signatures, policy disagreement and human-confirmed failures.
Playtest Question: is compute buying risk reduction or cosmetic variety?

### 7. Search-policy saturation differs from generator saturation
Observation: a search policy can become trapped in familiar neighborhoods.
Mechanism: parent/operator bias suppresses access to other regions.
Principle: challenge the search policy before declaring saturation.
Transfer: run a bounded challenge phase with fresh parents, alternate operators, unbiased seeds or descriptor reweighting.
Playtest Question: does novelty resume after policy perturbation?

### 8. Descriptor changes invalidate old saturation evidence
Observation: expressive-range conclusions depend on selected metrics and bins.
Mechanism: changing the map changes the meaning of “covered.”
Principle: saturation evidence is versioned.
Transfer: fingerprint generator, ruleset, descriptor schema, agent policy and severity model.
Playtest Question: did we change the map but keep the old “map is full” threshold?

### 9. Tail discovery needs uncertainty
Observation: rare discoveries make rolling slopes noisy.
Mechanism: a zero-novelty window can happen by chance.
Principle: treat marginal discovery rate as an estimate, not a fact.
Transfer: derive uncertainty bands from repeated historical windows/cohorts and require the upper bound to be low enough.
Playtest Question: is even the optimistic discovery-rate estimate below threshold?

### 10. Stopping is reversible
Observation: a new build, new human failure or new operator can reopen old regions.
Mechanism: saturation is conditional on current evidence and dependencies.
Principle: “stop” means stop optional compute under this evidence state.
Transfer: reopen exploration after semantic dependency changes or novel human-confirmed failures.
Playtest Question: what events invalidate the previous stop decision?

### 11. Canonical and unbiased floors are outside adaptive stopping
Observation: adaptive saturation says nothing about regressions or population drift.
Mechanism: frontier search and representative sampling answer different questions.
Principle: only the adaptive remainder may stop early.
Transfer: canonical fixtures and the declared unbiased sample floor always complete.
Playtest Question: did saturation accidentally remove longitudinal evidence?

### 12. Human confirmation calibrates severity and stopping together
Observation: machine metrics can disagree with player experience.
Mechanism: subtle comprehension failures may occupy ordinary metric cells.
Principle: machine and human actionable-discovery curves should both inform stopping.
Transfer: track promoted-case precision and human-found failure families absent from the archive.
Playtest Question: are humans still finding new consequential families after automation says saturated?

## Production calibration procedure
1. Collect multiple completed histories per generator/ruleset cohort.
2. Replay candidate stopping rules offline at many times T.
3. Continue each history to T+delta and label missed discoveries by severity/signature.
4. Measure compute saved, weighted cells missed, severe signatures missed, false-stop rate and time-to-next-actionable-discovery.
5. Select thresholds against an explicit miss-risk budget.
6. Validate on held-out campaigns/releases.
7. Runtime stopping requires canonical and unbiased floors, repeated low-yield windows, uncertainty agreement, no unresolved severe cluster, and a bounded challenge phase.
8. Persist the stop decision with semantic fingerprints so invalidation is deterministic.

## Dashboard additions
Track raw and consequence-weighted occupancy, reachable-state classification, marginal weighted coverage per compute-hour, severe-signature discovery per compute-hour, uncertainty bounds, time since last actionable discovery, historical false-stop rate, challenge-phase recovery rate, and human-found failure families absent from the automated archive.
