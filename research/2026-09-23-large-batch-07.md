# A. Arcade session and beginner onboarding

## A1. Difficulty is contextual

### Observation
Operator-oriented writing treated difficulty as something to tune for the people and location actually playing, not as a universal factory truth.

### Mechanism
A difficulty setting changes:
- first-success time
- average session length
- repeat-play likelihood
- intimidation

### Principle
**Tune difficulty against a target player and target session, not an abstract label.**

### Transfer
Mobile/Web:
- first session
- returning session
- challenge mode
can have different intended lengths.

---

## A2. A game can look too hard before the player ever touches it

### Observation
Spectators could be interested, watch an expert, and then decide the game looked too difficult or incomprehensible.

### Mechanism
Expert play demonstrates possibility but can also communicate an entry requirement far above a novice's perceived ability.

### Principle
**Attract content must communicate attainability as well as spectacle.**

---

## A3. Reading instructions is not equivalent to learning

### Observation
A proposed beginner mode slowed the game and gave enough time to try strategies rather than merely reading them.

### Mechanism
Motor and timing knowledge require action-feedback loops.

### Principle
**Teach by letting the player execute one safe success, not only by describing it.**

---

## A4. Continue systems preserve learning momentum

### Observation
Continue-from-progress systems were valued because they let players keep seeing and learning the game after failure.

### Mechanism
A total restart can convert a local failure into repeated low-skill traversal.

### Principle
**Separate mastery challenges from access to later learning.**

Score modes can still reward no-continue play.

---

## A5. Finite and endless structures serve different motivations

### Observation
Period discussion showed disagreement over whether a game should be finishable; one side emphasized masters wanting indefinite play, another newcomers being discouraged by no possible end.

### Mechanism
A visible endpoint reduces perceived commitment, while endless play creates a mastery ceiling that never closes.

### Principle
**Offer a reachable completion goal, then layer endless/mastery goals when appropriate.**

---

## A6. Thresholds must look reachable

### Observation
Operator analysis warned that extremely high visible target scores could discourage players who did not believe they had a chance.

### Mechanism
A reward target only motivates when the player can imagine a path toward it.

### Principle
**Progress goals should stretch the player without looking mathematically irrelevant.**

---

## A7. Beginner assistance can be physical, not numeric

### Observation
Pinball analysis noted that a layout demanding precise skill with little incidental rebound or mistake recovery could be harsh for beginners.

### Mechanism
Small passive recoveries allow novices to stay in the interaction long enough to learn.

### Principle
**Beginner support can come from forgiving geometry and recovery opportunities, not only extra HP.**

---

# B. Fighting game depth

## B1. Move lists are only the vocabulary

### Observation
Historical strategy material separated move execution from character-specific head-to-head tactics.

### Mechanism
Knowing an input is not the same as knowing:
- when it is safe
- what it counters
- what range it controls
- what state follows

### Principle
**Fighting depth lives in application context, not command count.**

---

## B2. Character identity is relational

### Observation
One-on-one strategy changes by opponent.

### Mechanism
A character's strength is not absolute. Range, counter options and risk change against another toolset.

### Principle
**Balance matchups pairwise, not only character-by-character.**

---

## B3. Throws matter because they rewrite space

### Observation
Throw direction and range are strategically important beyond damage.

### Mechanism
A throw can:
- switch sides
- create corner pressure
- reset neutral
- start wake-up pressure

### Principle
**Evaluate moves by the next spatial state they create.**

---

## B4. Strong patterns become interesting when counterplay exists

### Observation
Strategy literature spends substantial space on answers to strong or frustrating tactics.

### Mechanism
Counterplay changes an annoying pattern into a question:
> Will they repeat it, and am I willing to call it out?

### Principle
**Powerful tactics need discoverable, proportionate answers.**

---

## B5. Training should recreate the problem

### Observation
Learning advanced play involves practicing responses to situations, not merely repeating special-move execution.

### Mechanism
The shortest learning loop is:
failure state → immediate recreation → alternate response.

### Principle
**Training tools should be scenario editors, not only move checklists.**

---

# C. Racing and vehicle games

## C1. High speed requires a longer information horizon

### Observation
Racing criticism praised unusual low viewpoints for excitement, but other racing criticism penalized sharp turns with insufficient warning.

### Mechanism
At higher speed, the same visual distance represents less reaction time.

### Principle
**Increase preview distance or environmental signaling as speed rises.**

---

## C2. Rivals need to physically affect the line

### Observation
A racer could feel less exciting when opponents were rarely visible and rarely forced side-by-side position fights.

### Mechanism
Visible rivals turn a time trial into:
- blocking
- passing
- defending
- route compromise

### Principle
**Racing AI should change the player's line, not merely the leaderboard.**

---

## C3. Vehicle feedback is information

### Observation
Historical driving reviews praised moving cabinets, steering resistance, vibration and camera motion as part of the driving experience.

### Mechanism
Physical feedback can communicate:
- contact
- surface
- slip
- speed
without requiring HUD attention.

### Principle
**Use haptics/audio/camera as state sensors, not decoration.**

---

## C4. Route choice multiplies replay without control bloat

### Observation
Branching road structures let players see different destinations while preserving one driving model.

### Mechanism
The player reuses mastered controls in a new context.

### Principle
**Branch content before adding control complexity.**

---

## C5. Post-run route visualization advertises unexplored content

### Observation
Showing the path taken makes visible that other paths exist.

### Mechanism
A result screen can transform unseen content into an explicit future goal.

### Principle
**Results can teach the player what they have not yet experienced.**

---

# D. Shooter recovery and encounter structure

## D1. Power loss makes death a persistent difficulty modifier

### Observation
Classic shooters with checkpoint restarts and lost power-ups are often remembered as especially punishing because recovery can be harder than the original approach.

### Mechanism
Death changes the player's capability while the stage expectation may remain calibrated for high power.

### Principle
**Test every recovery point with weak equipment, not only ideal equipment.**

---

## D2. One death can create a failure cascade

### Observation
When power loss sharply lowers damage or mobility, the next section becomes harder, increasing the chance of another death.

### Mechanism
A local mistake compounds into an extended downward spiral.

### Principle
**Provide a credible recovery path after a mistake.**

---

## D3. Restart style changes the rhythm of mastery

### Observation
Respawn-in-place and checkpoint restart create fundamentally different learning loops.

### Mechanism
- in-place: preserves encounter rhythm, tests improvisation
- checkpoint: emphasizes memorized section mastery

### Principle
**Choose restart structure according to what skill the game wants to teach.**

---

## D4. Power-up choices should interact with level geometry

### Observation
Weapon options in classic shooters can have different effectiveness depending on terrain and enemy direction.

### Mechanism
Weapon choice becomes a prediction about the upcoming environment.

### Principle
**Power-ups are deeper when stage topology changes their value.**

---

## D5. Encounter density needs phrasing

### Observation
Strong shooters are often described not simply by enemy quantity but by varied foes and techniques.

### Mechanism
Repeated equal-density pressure becomes noise; changing threat roles creates readable phrases.

### Principle
**Design enemy waves as sequences with setup, combination, climax and release.**

---

# E. Puzzle planning

## E1. Preview changes reaction into planning

### Observation
Falling-block puzzle design benefits from showing an upcoming piece.

### Mechanism
The current move can now be judged against a future constraint.

### Principle
**Expose enough future state to make multi-step decisions possible.**

---

## E2. Time pressure can deepen a stable ruleset

### Observation
A simple ruleset can remain compelling while execution speed rises.

### Mechanism
The player is not learning new rules; they are compressing recognition and planning into less time.

### Principle
**Mastery can come from faster application of stable rules.**

---

## E3. Player-controlled speed creates voluntary difficulty

### Observation
Faster placement can reward players who willingly sacrifice correction time.

### Mechanism
Expert players convert spare cognitive capacity into score or tempo.

### Principle
**Let skilled players choose to accelerate the game for additional reward.**

---

## E4. Sequels should add a planning axis, not replace the core

### Observation
Puzzle sequels have added rising terrain, special pieces and multiplayer structures while retaining the foundational placement problem.

### Mechanism
The original skill remains useful, but its context becomes richer.

### Principle
**Expand the decision graph rather than discard the learned grammar.**

---

# F. RPG economy, repetition, and interface

## F1. Currency is only meaningful while choices compete

### Observation
RPG criticism repeatedly notices when money becomes plentiful but lacks meaningful late-game uses.

### Mechanism
Once every desired purchase is affordable, currency stops creating decisions.

### Principle
**Maintain multiple desirable sinks across the whole progression.**

---

## F2. Training costs can give money a long-term purpose

### Observation
Some RPG structures keep gold relevant by tying progression itself to recurring expenditure.

### Mechanism
Loot, risk and character growth become one economic loop.

### Principle
**Recurring sinks are stronger than one-time shop tiers, provided they create choices rather than taxes.**

---

## F3. Convenience abilities can be meaningful progression

### Observation
Teleport/shortcut abilities can remove already-solved traversal.

### Mechanism
The player's reward is not stronger combat but reduced repetition.

### Principle
**Progression can grant freedom from previously mastered friction.**

---

## F4. Repetitive interactions can be more damaging than difficult ones

### Observation
Long sequences of repeated door opening, item management or menu navigation are criticized when they add little strategy.

### Mechanism
A tiny friction multiplied hundreds of times becomes a major share of playtime.

### Principle
**Audit high-frequency actions by total lifetime cost.**

---

## F5. Interface convenience must scale with system depth

### Observation
Complex RPGs and simulations can remain approachable when frequent actions have direct inputs or shortcuts.

### Mechanism
Depth should come from deciding, not from navigating to the decision.

### Principle
**As systemic complexity rises, interaction overhead should shrink.**

---

# G. Simulation feedback

## G1. Abstract systems require visible consequences

### Observation
City/simulation play becomes engaging when buildings, traffic and conditions visibly react to decisions.

### Mechanism
Visual/system feedback lets the player infer causal rules.

### Principle
**Make simulation state changes observable soon enough to support hypothesis testing.**

---

## G2. Slow causality needs intermediate signals

### Observation
Long-term systems cannot always produce instant final outcomes.

### Mechanism
Without intermediate data, a player cannot distinguish “wrong strategy” from “not enough time yet.”

### Principle
**Expose leading indicators when final outcomes are delayed.**

---

# H. Cross-domain synthesis

## H1. Every game needs a recovery design, not only a failure design

Failure asks:
> What happens when the player is wrong?

Recovery asks:
> What meaningful decisions let them become competitive again?

This applies to:
- shooter power loss
- racing spin-outs
- fighting knockdowns
- RPG resource depletion
- strategy economic collapse

---

## H2. A game's hardest content should be easier to retry, not harder

As mastery requirements rise, repeated low-skill overhead should fall.

Examples:
- shorter restart
- scenario training
- checkpoint near challenge
- faster menu access

Difficulty should concentrate on the target skill.

---

## H3. Visibility has three audiences

Design visual communication for:
1. player currently acting
2. novice deciding whether to participate
3. spectator / teammate interpreting state

A presentation can succeed for one and fail for another.

---

## H4. Design metrics need segmentation

Averages can hide two opposite failures.

Example:
- novices quit at 20 seconds
- experts play 20 minutes
- “average session” looks acceptable

Record behavior by experience level and session context.
