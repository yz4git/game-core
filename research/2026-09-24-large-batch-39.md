# Large Batch 39 — Validity-preserving mutation and boundary search

Date: 2026-09-24

## Scope

Coverage review showed that procedural QA already had canonical/random/adversarial cohorts, behavior-space saturation, failure shrinking and semantic visual regression. The thin high-priority area was **mutation operators that stay inside the valid-content manifold while deliberately approaching player-relevant failure boundaries**.

This batch therefore asks a narrower production question: once a generator can make valid content, how should QA mutate a valid seed so that it finds fragile-but-still-playable cases instead of wasting most of its budget on obviously broken garbage?

Historical cross-sample used public Gaming Alexandria material across eras and genres: Micom BASIC 1983–1985 preserved shooters/action/puzzle material; Program Pochette 1984–1985 maze/action/shooter/puzzle material; Technopolis Aug 1987; GamePro 1989; Play Meter Aug/Nov 1990; Famimaga 64 Dec 1996 and the complete-set context; plus the 1996 Micom BASIC Battle Baller example. These are used only as comparative design evidence; no magazine prose, scans or long quotations are stored here.

Modern comparison material included simulation-based PCG playability testing (Shaker et al., AIIDE 2013), coverage-guided property-based testing, property-based counterexample shrinking, and structured mutation/fuzzing practice.

## Source observations used

- Early type-in games frequently expose compact rule systems with hard resource, life, movement, timing or spatial constraints. Preserved examples include limited-ammunition aiming games, maze collection under pursuit, sliding puzzles, two-player space combat, score/time pressure and course time trials.
- Gaming Alexandria's Program Pochette project preserves all 17 issues from 1984–1987 and describes the series as a venue for submitted programs across many Japanese computer families.
- Micom BASIC preservation spans very different machines and genres; preserved programs make implementation constraints and playable rule boundaries unusually visible.
- Later magazine material broadens the context from compact home-computer rule systems to arcade operation, console-era 3D and performance/competition expectations.
- Simulation-based PCG research shows that generated content needs an executable playability test; restricting search to sensible actions can make otherwise intractable continuous spaces testable.
- Coverage-guided property-based testing highlights a core problem relevant to PCG: syntactically generated cases are often useless when important semantic invariants are rare.
- Property-based testing's shrinking idea is useful after a failure is found, but PCG also needs the dual operation before failure: validity-preserving mutation toward the boundary.

## New Observation / Mechanism candidates

### 1. Invalid-output rate is itself a search-quality metric
**Observation:** A naive adversarial mutator can appear productive while spending most executions on disconnected roads, impossible key orders, overlapping spawns or other trivial invalidity.

**Mechanism:** The search objective rewards a failure score without accounting for whether the candidate remained inside the generator's legal manifold.

**Generalized Principle:** Measure `valid offspring / attempted offspring` per mutation operator. An adversarial operator that mostly produces immediate hard-invalid cases is consuming QA budget, not exploring gameplay risk.

**Transfer:** Road generation, dungeon graphs, racing courses, puzzle boards, encounter layouts, loot/economy generation.

**Playtest Question:** Which operator finds the most *new severe valid or near-valid cases per 1,000 simulations*, rather than the most malformed cases?

### 2. Mutate semantic decisions, not serialized bytes
**Observation:** Many generated artifacts have strong relationships: road endpoints belong to junctions, keys belong before locks, enemies belong to encounter budgets, puzzle pieces obey counts/parity, and cover belongs to navigable surfaces.

**Mechanism:** Byte/coordinate-level perturbation destroys these relationships before it reaches interesting gameplay states.

**Generalized Principle:** Mutation vocabulary should operate on domain semantics: move junction, swap branch role, redistribute encounter budget, shift resource timing, substitute enemy role, widen/narrow timing window, rotate room connector, alter landmark reveal point.

**Transfer:** Prefer `move objective to another reachable branch` over `randomize objective x/y`.

**Playtest Question:** Can each high-value mutation be described in gameplay language?

### 3. Separate repairable invariants from sacred invariants
**Observation:** Some constraints may be repaired after mutation without changing the experiment; others define the experiment itself.

**Mechanism:** A generic repair pass can silently undo the mutation or introduce unrelated changes.

**Generalized Principle:** Classify invariants as **sacred** (must never break), **repairable** (may be restored locally), and **measured** (allowed to approach/fail because it is the target). Record every repair.

**Transfer:** In a road-width stress test, connectivity may be sacred, lane marking repairable, and minimum clearance measured.

**Playtest Question:** Did the repair preserve the intended perturbation, or did it create a different test case?

### 4. Use constrained projection after mutation
**Observation:** Small semantic edits can still push a candidate just outside legality.

**Mechanism:** Rejecting every such candidate loses useful direction information; fully regenerating it loses locality.

**Generalized Principle:** After mutation, project the candidate to the nearest legal state under explicitly allowed repairs. Bound repair distance and reject cases that require large projection.

**Transfer:** Snap a moved spawn to the nearest valid nav surface; restore a minimum corridor width; reconnect one severed edge without rewriting the route graph.

**Playtest Question:** What is the repair distance, and at what point is the result no longer meaningfully descended from its parent?

### 5. Boundary search needs a margin vector, not pass/fail
**Observation:** Two playable seeds can both pass while one has 40% jump slack and the other 1%.

**Mechanism:** Boolean validity erases direction toward fragile cases.

**Generalized Principle:** Export a vector of signed margins: clearance, reaction time, resource slack, alternate-route cost, unavoidable damage, visibility-before-decision, puzzle move surplus, recovery distance. Search toward zero while retaining validity.

**Transfer:** Use lexicographic or Pareto search rather than one weighted score when margins represent different failure modes.

**Playtest Question:** Which valid seeds sit closest to multiple independent boundaries at once?

### 6. Compound near-boundaries are more valuable than single extremes
**Observation:** Production failures often occur when individually acceptable factors combine: slightly narrow road + slightly late landmark + moderate traffic; low ammo + long encounter + poor recovery pickup.

**Mechanism:** One-dimensional stress tests miss interactions.

**Generalized Principle:** Once single-margin boundaries are mapped, deliberately cross-breed or mutate cases toward two- and three-margin intersections.

**Transfer:** Maintain a boundary-pair matrix and count discovered interaction failures.

**Playtest Question:** Which pairs of margins predict human failure better than either margin alone?

### 7. Mutation step size should adapt to acceptance and novelty
**Observation:** Tiny mutations can waste time rediscovering the same behavior cell; large mutations fall off the valid manifold.

**Mechanism:** Fixed mutation magnitude cannot serve both exploration and boundary refinement.

**Generalized Principle:** Adapt step size using valid-offspring rate, behavior novelty and margin improvement. Expand when offspring remain valid but behavior is stagnant; contract when validity collapses or a boundary is being refined.

**Transfer:** Per-operator step-size controllers are preferable to one global mutation strength.

**Playtest Question:** Is each operator producing a useful balance of validity, novelty and boundary progress?

### 8. Parent selection should reward information gain
**Observation:** Keeping only the numerically worst seed causes search collapse around one bug family.

**Mechanism:** Elite-only selection overexploits a local failure basin.

**Generalized Principle:** Parent selection should preserve representatives from behavior cells, failure signatures, margin-frontier regions and human-disagreement clusters.

**Transfer:** Use archive-based selection similar to quality-diversity: keep distinct informative parents even when their raw severity is lower.

**Playtest Question:** How many independent failure mechanisms, not just failing seeds, are currently represented in the parent pool?

### 9. Human traces can define mutation axes the generator never exposed
**Observation:** Players reveal latent variables such as hesitation before a junction, repeated camera correction, emergency braking, backtracking, inventory thrashing or repeated failed approaches.

**Mechanism:** Generator parameters may not directly encode the cause of those behaviors.

**Generalized Principle:** Convert recurring trace patterns into derived descriptors, then mutate upstream content toward the descriptor boundary even if no single generator knob corresponds to it.

**Transfer:** Search for increased pre-junction steering entropy rather than merely changing road angle.

**Playtest Question:** Which human complaints still lack a machine-measurable descriptor or mutation route?

### 10. Preserve ancestry and delta provenance
**Observation:** A failing child is far more diagnosable when its differences from a known-good parent are small and explicit.

**Mechanism:** Seed-only reproduction says *what* failed but not *which semantic change exposed it*.

**Generalized Principle:** Store parent ID, operator, semantic delta, repair delta, generator/build version and resulting margin vector for every retained adversarial case.

**Transfer:** This turns adversarial search into a causal debugging aid rather than only a bug lottery.

**Playtest Question:** Can a developer explain a retained failure as a short chain of semantic mutations from a known-good fixture?

### 11. Shrink in the same semantic vocabulary used to mutate
**Observation:** Generic shrinking can remove geometry or entities until the artifact no longer resembles a legal production case.

**Mechanism:** The shrinker and generator disagree about valid structure.

**Generalized Principle:** Pair each mutation operator with inverse/simplifying operations that preserve sacred invariants. Minimize semantic delta first, raw object count second.

**Transfer:** Reduce traffic count, then road curvature delta, then landmark offset while keeping the same deadline-occlusion failure.

**Playtest Question:** Is the minimized repro still a valid member of the production content family?

### 12. Mutation effectiveness must survive generator revisions
**Observation:** A generator update can make yesterday's operator ineffective without changing its code.

**Mechanism:** Parameter sensitivity and legal manifolds move as generation rules evolve.

**Generalized Principle:** Version mutation-operator telemetry and monitor valid-offspring rate, novel-cell yield, severe-failure yield and median repair distance across releases.

**Transfer:** Treat large operator-yield shifts as generator-regression signals.

**Playtest Question:** Which mutation operators lost reach after the latest generator change, and what behavior region disappeared with them?

## Production pipeline

`KNOWN-GOOD PARENT → SEMANTIC MUTATION → SACRED-INVARIANT CHECK → BOUNDED LOCAL REPAIR → STATIC VALIDATION → MARGIN VECTOR → DYNAMIC SIMULATION → BEHAVIOR/FALIURE SIGNATURE → ARCHIVE OR DISCARD → SEMANTIC SHRINK → REGRESSION FIXTURE`

For every operator record:

- attempts
- valid-before-repair rate
- valid-after-bounded-repair rate
- median / p95 repair distance
- new behavior cells per 1k attempts
- new boundary cells per 1k attempts
- new severe failure signatures per 1k simulations
- duplicate failure ratio
- human-confirmed issue precision
- average simulation/render cost

## Genre-specific operator examples

### Shooter
- preserve spawn reachability while shifting enemy arrival overlap
- redistribute enemy-role budget rather than adding arbitrary enemies
- move recovery pickup later while preserving theoretical completion
- tighten safe-lane duration toward reaction-time boundary

### Racing
- perturb curvature/grade/width locally while preserving route connectivity
- move landmark reveal timing toward braking/steering deadline
- alter traffic phase while preserving lane legality
- reduce alternate-route margin without disconnecting the network

### RPG / dungeon
- move key/resource/NPC among reachable prerequisite-equivalent nodes
- redistribute encounter/resource pressure along a valid progression path
- substitute enemy roles at equal budget to search composition interactions
- increase backtracking cost while preserving quest reachability

### Puzzle
- mutate clue/restriction placement while retaining at least one solver-confirmed solution
- reduce solution slack toward uniqueness or move-count boundary
- alter distractors without changing the intended rule set
- preserve parity/count invariants during board mutation

## Release gate proposal

Do not accept “10k adversarial seeds tested” by itself. Require evidence that:

1. mutation operators retain a healthy valid-offspring rate;
2. boundary coverage spans multiple margin dimensions;
3. severe failures are deduplicated by mechanism/signature;
4. compound-boundary search is represented;
5. retained failures have ancestry and semantic deltas;
6. minimized repros remain production-valid;
7. human-confirmed failures feed back into descriptors/operators.
