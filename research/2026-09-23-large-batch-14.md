# Observation / Mechanism candidates

## 1. Difficulty can be a location parameter, not only a player parameter

### Observation
Historical coin-op guidance explicitly recommended different initial difficulty for different venue audiences, with later adjustment based on observed play time and revenue.

### Mechanism
A bar, family arcade, expert venue and temporary event expose the same game to different prior skill, patience and social context. One universal factory setting therefore optimizes for no particular audience.

### Generalized Principle
**Tune difficulty against the audience context and observed session distribution, not only an abstract Easy/Normal/Hard ladder.**

### Transfer
- arcade mode presets
- public demo builds
- convention builds
- onboarding cohorts
- adaptive first-session difficulty

### Playtest Question
Does the same build produce a useful first-session success/failure distribution for the actual audience that will encounter it?

---

## 2. Session length is an operational variable with a target band

### Observation
Coin-op trade material discussed target average game duration and repeat play together rather than treating survival time as an unlimited good.

### Mechanism
Too-short sessions feel unfair and prevent learning; excessively long sessions reduce turnover and may remove the desire for a clean second attempt. The useful target depends on business model and social setting.

### Generalized Principle
**Optimize session length for a repeatable learning loop, not for maximum duration.**

### Transfer
- score attack
- YouTube Playables
- event demos
- mobile quick sessions
- roguelite rooms

### Playtest Question
After one session, is the player satisfied enough to stop but informed enough to immediately want another try?

---

## 3. Attract mode can teach strategy before payment or commitment

### Observation
Play Meter examples praised attract sequences that paired instructions with corresponding playfield cues; other machines exposed optional attract audio and visual hooks.

### Mechanism
A spectator can acquire a mental model before taking control. This lowers the cost of the first paid/committed attempt and converts watching into onboarding.

### Generalized Principle
**Use idle/demo presentation to teach the first meaningful decision, not merely to show spectacle.**

### Transfer
- title-screen demos
- lobby previews
- tutorial GIFs
- store-page clips
- spectator mode

### Playtest Question
Can a non-player watch the demo for 20–30 seconds and correctly describe the first thing they should try?

---

## 4. Operator controls are a historical form of live tuning

### Observation
Coin-op machines exposed difficulty, bonus thresholds, time-per-coin, pricing, payout and audit controls to operators.

### Mechanism
The product was not a single immutable ruleset; it was a configurable system adjusted against local behavior and economics.

### Generalized Principle
**Separate designer intent from deploy-time tuning variables when context can materially change the optimum.**

### Transfer
- remote config
- event presets
- cohort experiments
- server-side economy knobs
- accessibility presets

### Playtest Question
Which balance variables genuinely need deployment-time adjustment, and which would make the rules feel inconsistent if changed remotely?

---

## 5. Price, difficulty and play time form one coupled system

### Observation
Historical operator material records changes in play count when price changed, and warns that fixed pricing could become commercially inflexible as novelty declined.

### Mechanism
Price changes the player's tolerance for failure and expected session value; difficulty changes duration; duration changes throughput and perceived value.

### Generalized Principle
**Never tune monetization, difficulty and session duration as independent systems when they directly affect one another.**

### Transfer
Even in non-coin games, substitute:
- ad exposure
- energy cost
- queue time
- retry token
- event ticket
for physical coin cost.

### Playtest Question
When the retry cost rises, does the game still give enough information and agency for failure to feel worth paying/retrying?

---

## 6. Public play creates a spectator-to-player conversion funnel

### Observation
Hokra's development emphasized readable trails, simple rules, visible score and short matches after observing that many more people watched than played.

### Mechanism
Short rounds reduce waiting cost while strong motion traces and obvious scoring let newcomers understand the state without explanation.

### Generalized Principle
**In public/shared games, design the waiting player as a future participant.**

### Transfer
- local multiplayer
- arcade cabinets
- party games
- esports side events
- game-show modes

### Playtest Question
How long does a first-time spectator need before they can predict what a player is trying to do?

---

## 7. Spectator readability benefits from persistent motion history

### Observation
Player trails in Hokra made movement easier to read for people who were not already tracking every avatar.

### Mechanism
A moving object's current position does not explain where it came from. Short-lived trails externalize recent history without requiring replay.

### Generalized Principle
**When velocity or route matters, expose a small amount of recent history visually.**

### Transfer
- racing racing-line ghosts
- projectile traces
- dodge direction
- sports passing lanes
- tactical movement

### Playtest Question
Can a viewer infer recent direction and momentum from one frozen moment of the screen?

---

## 8. Photo mode becomes deeper when it exposes authored world variables

### Observation
A Highland Song's photo mode lets players manipulate variables such as weather/lighting that already drive the game's art system, while Dredge connected photography to an in-world character and wildlife discovery.

### Mechanism
The mode stops being a detached screenshot utility and becomes a second way to interact with existing art, exploration and narrative systems.

### Generalized Principle
**A strong photo mode reuses the game's expressive variables and world goals instead of only unlocking a free camera.**

### Transfer
- replay editor
- diorama mode
- character pose mode
- world photography quests
- shareable challenge cards

### Playtest Question
Does taking a photo require the player to notice or manipulate something meaningful about this game's world?

---

## 9. Photo mode can preserve contextual memory, not only pixels

### Observation
A Highland Song retains recent dialogue for use in compositions; Dredge's planned implementation tied camera use to wildlife clues and world discovery.

### Mechanism
A screenshot becomes more personally meaningful when it can carry a remembered line, event, weather state or discovered subject.

### Generalized Principle
**Capture systems can save a thin layer of gameplay context alongside the image.**

### Transfer
- run recap cards
- RPG travel journals
- boss victory photos
- racing highlight cards

### Playtest Question
Months later, does the captured artifact remind the player what happened, or only what the scene looked like?

---

## 10. Procedural variety should be measured structurally, not only by seed uniqueness

### Observation
Generator-visualization work can reveal repeated rising/falling patterns or chaotic detail even when individual levels are technically different. Unexplored instead builds around gameplay cycles before spatial realization.

### Mechanism
Different random seeds can still produce the same experiential grammar. Conversely, a shared structural grammar can generate coherent variations when the cycle itself changes meaningfully.

### Generalized Principle
**Measure procedural diversity in gameplay structure and spatial rhythm, not only asset arrangement or hash uniqueness.**

### Transfer
- WorldSeed districts
- roguelike rooms
- racing routes
- encounter generators

### Playtest Question
Can players distinguish two seeds by the decisions they made, not merely by where objects appeared?

---

## 11. Procedural generation should encode a playable grammar before decoration

### Observation
Cyclic dungeon generation constructs gameplay relations before translating them into space; other procedural systems separate structural placement from later decorative passes.

### Mechanism
If decoration and topology are generated together without hierarchy, visual novelty can hide repetitive or incoherent decision structure.

### Generalized Principle
**Generate decision structure first, spatial realization second, decoration third.**

### Transfer
- city roads → blocks → props
- dungeon goals → paths → rooms → dressing
- shooter encounters → cover topology → enemy dressing

### Playtest Question
If all decorative assets are replaced with grey boxes, do generated seeds still produce meaningfully different play?

---

## 12. Operational telemetry predates digital analytics—and the lesson still applies

### Observation
Coin-op equipment exposed meters, audits and bookkeeping so operators could compare plays, payouts and earnings and retune settings.

### Mechanism
Without outcome measurement, configurable difficulty/pricing becomes guesswork.

### Generalized Principle
**Every deploy-time tuning knob should have an observable metric that tells you whether changing it helped.**

### Transfer
- difficulty cohort telemetry
- retry rate
- session length
- completion rate
- queue abandonment
- economy sinks

### Playtest Question
For each live-tunable variable, what metric would make you reverse the change?

- greybox generated cities to check whether district/route decisions survive without decoration

### Break Cars / Sky Dancer

- highlight cards can preserve run context, not only images
