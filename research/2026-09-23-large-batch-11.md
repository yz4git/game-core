# Observation / Mechanism candidates

## 1. Sports games should preserve the sport’s decision loop, not every physical motion

### Observation
Early magazine programs reduce sports to tiny input sets, while later sports experiments show that a convincing sports feel can survive radical abstraction when timing, positioning, anticipation and contest remain.

### Mechanism
Literal motion reproduction increases input burden faster than it increases meaningful decisions. Players accept abstraction once the input-to-outcome relationship is stable and readable.

### Generalized Principle
**Simulate the decisions that define the sport before simulating all of its body mechanics.**

### Transfer
A football game may preserve space creation, passing lanes and timing with only a few commands. A racing game may preserve line choice and braking without modelling every mechanical subsystem.

### Playtest Question
If animation and branding were replaced by simple shapes, would players still make recognizably sport-specific decisions?

---

## 2. Camera orientation and control mapping form one learned coordinate system

### Observation
Sports camera experiments show that a more broadcast-authentic camera can feel worse when established controls were learned around another orientation.

### Mechanism
Players do not learn camera and controls independently. Directional input becomes a mental transform tied to the default view.

### Generalized Principle
**Treat camera changes as control-system changes, not cosmetic presentation changes.**

### Transfer
When adding alternate cameras to driving, football, flying or action games, retest steering, target switching and directional gestures under every view.

### Playtest Question
Does the same directional input feel equally predictable after switching camera orientation?

---

## 3. Team-sport AI is judged by coordinated intent, not individual animation quality

### Observation
Sports AI can have excellent body physics yet still feel wrong when teammates fail to coordinate positioning, assignments or play structure.

### Mechanism
The player reads teammates as parts of a plan. A locally plausible movement that contradicts team intent is globally implausible.

### Generalized Principle
**Evaluate multi-agent AI at formation/team level before polishing individual-agent realism.**

### Transfer
Applicable to football, squad shooters, RTS formations and party RPG companions.

### Playtest Question
Can the player explain what the AI team is trying to accomplish as a group?

---

## 4. Sports complexity should be layered by situation

### Observation
Telemetry from Madden indicated novice benefit from simpler playbooks and clearer in-game controls, while expert play depends on deeper tactical choice.

### Mechanism
Presenting the complete tactical vocabulary before the player has a situation requiring it converts depth into menu search.

### Generalized Principle
**Expose the minimum viable tactical choice first; reveal formation, matchup and specialist detail as the player gains context.**

### Transfer
Quick Play can expose a small recommended set while advanced modes retain full tactical control.

### Playtest Question
How many choices must a first-time player parse before the first meaningful play begins?

---

## 5. Narrative consequence creates planning only when the player can form a causal model

### Observation
A choice that appears to produce the same result removes the player’s ability to plan, even if hidden state changes internally.

### Mechanism
Planning requires an understandable relationship between action and future state. Invisible consequence is often experienced as no consequence.

### Generalized Principle
**Important choices need observable evidence that the world remembered them, even when the final consequence is delayed.**

### Transfer
Use changed dialogue, availability, relationship state, environment or intermediate outcomes before a delayed payoff.

### Playtest Question
After a major choice, can the player identify at least one state that changed because of it?

---

## 6. Irreversibility and experimentation are separate design axes

### Observation
Narrative games variously restrict saves, embrace rewind/metagaming, or allow a narrow immediate correction window.

### Mechanism
No-takeback rules increase commitment but can also punish accidental input or misunderstood wording. Full rewind supports exploration but changes choices into information gathering.

### Generalized Principle
**Choose deliberately whether a narrative choice tests commitment, discovery, or optimization; configure save/rewind rules to match that purpose.**

### Transfer
A committed detective game can autosave after decisions but still allow immediate correction for accidental selection; an exploratory time-loop game can make replaying consequences the core mechanic.

### Playtest Question
When players reload, are they correcting an interface mistake, exploring possibility space, or erasing an unwanted consequence?

---

## 7. Delayed consequences need intermediate acknowledgment

### Observation
Long-delay choices can feel meaningless until payoff if the game offers no sign that the choice entered world state.

### Mechanism
Human causal attribution weakens when action and response are far apart and nothing bridges them.

### Generalized Principle
**For delayed consequences, provide a short-term acknowledgment without necessarily revealing the final result.**

### Transfer
NPC reaction, journal change, faction mood, altered route availability or subtle world-state feedback can preserve uncertainty while confirming causality.

### Playtest Question
Can players distinguish “consequence intentionally delayed” from “the game ignored my choice”?

---

## 8. Speedrun-friendly design benefits ordinary retries too

### Observation
Speedrunning communities value fast restarts, deterministic behavior, skippable downtime and stable movement; these also reduce friction for normal mastery play.

### Mechanism
High-skill iteration depends on shortening the loop between hypothesis, attempt, failure and retry.

### Generalized Principle
**Treat restart latency as part of challenge difficulty.**

### Transfer
Boss rush, score attack, racing time trial, rhythm practice and difficult platforming all benefit.

### Playtest Question
How many seconds and inputs separate a failed attempt from the next meaningful input?

---

## 9. Optional skips can become mastery routes instead of bugs

### Observation
Some shipped games deliberately retain routes that allow skilled players to bypass encounters; speedrunning communities also turn unusual movement and route knowledge into long-term mastery.

### Mechanism
A skip creates a risk/reward route when it requires knowledge or execution and does not destroy competitive fairness.

### Generalized Principle
**Do not automatically remove sequence breaks; classify whether they create expressive mastery, trivialize the game, or cause instability.**

### Transfer
Use explicit exploit classification already present in Game Core, adding speedrun-route value as a criterion.

### Playtest Question
Does the skip require repeatable skill/knowledge, and what meaningful content or risk is traded for the time saved?

---

## 10. Determinism should be scoped to what mastery depends on

### Observation
Challenge play becomes hard to learn when identical inputs encounter materially different outcomes, but complete determinism can remove adaptation.

### Mechanism
Players need stable causal rules to optimize; variation is useful when it changes the problem rather than invalidating learned execution.

### Generalized Principle
**Keep movement, collision and timing deterministic enough for mastery; place variation in readable scenario selection where adaptation is the intended skill.**

### Transfer
For procedural speedrun modes, expose seed/category and separate fixed-seed records from random-seed adaptation records.

### Playtest Question
When a record attempt fails, can the runner attribute it to execution/decision rather than hidden simulation variance?

---

## 11. Strategy automation should remove execution volume, not strategic ownership

### Observation
Northgard intentionally automates or semi-automates many actions to emphasize atmosphere and long-term strategy; The Colonists automates worker path/task execution while leaving network and production structure to the player.

### Mechanism
Micromanagement is valuable only when the repeated command itself expresses strategy. Otherwise it consumes attention that could be spent on higher-order choices.

### Generalized Principle
**Automate actions whose optimal execution is obvious; keep control where competing goals make the decision meaningful.**

### Transfer
Workers can choose routes automatically while the player designs roads; units can maintain formation while the player chooses formation and objective.

### Playtest Question
If this repeated command were automated perfectly, which meaningful decision would disappear?

---

## 12. Automation must remain legible

### Observation
The Colonists makes automated resource movement visible in-world; tactical games emphasize clarity of AI actions; strategy AI architectures separate macro intent from micro execution.

### Mechanism
Automation saves input but can destroy agency when players cannot predict why it acted or how to influence it.

### Generalized Principle
**Every important automated system needs visible intent, state and override points.**

### Transfer
Show destination, task queue, formation target, production priority, route or reason-for-idle rather than only final motion.

### Playtest Question
Before the automation completes, can the player predict what it will do and intervene if needed?

---

## 13. AI personality should be expressed through strategic priorities, not hidden stat bonuses

### Observation
Halo Wars 2 encodes recognizable macro styles such as rush, expansion, tech and defense, then combines them with micro behaviours.

### Mechanism
Players learn an opponent when repeated decisions reveal a policy. Pure numeric advantages make difficulty stronger but not more interpretable.

### Generalized Principle
**Give AI opponents distinct priority structures and decision policies before giving them invisible advantages.**

### Transfer
Racing rivals can prefer late braking or defensive lines; RPG enemies can prioritize control, sustain or burst; strategy factions can prefer expansion or fortification.

### Playtest Question
Can players identify an AI personality from its choices without seeing its name or stat modifiers?

---

## 14. Tactical clarity limits how much AI cleverness can be shown at once

### Observation
When many AI agents act, camera motion and simultaneous action can make a tactically correct turn unreadable.

### Mechanism
The player cannot learn from AI decisions that occur faster than attention can follow them.

### Generalized Principle
**AI action scheduling and presentation bandwidth are coupled; intelligence that cannot be perceived does not improve the experience.**

### Transfer
Sequence high-priority enemy actions, group low-value movement, minimize unnecessary camera pans and preserve threat hierarchy.

### Playtest Question
After an AI turn, can the player recount the two or three decisions that mattered most?

---

## 15. Automated play agents can turn repetitive balance checking into measurable coverage

### Observation
Racing development can use learned agents to test car/track combinations and visualize difficult regions instead of relying entirely on repeated manual laps.

### Mechanism
Machines are useful for exhaustive consistency checks; humans remain necessary for feel, readability and fun.

### Generalized Principle
**Use automated agents to find outliers and coverage gaps, then spend human playtest time on qualitative judgment.**

### Transfer
WorldSeed: driveability checks; racing: car × track matrices; procgen: seed feasibility; strategy: economy deadlocks.

### Playtest Question
Which repetitive validation task could an agent perform thousands of times so humans can inspect only the suspicious cases?

---

## 16. Small historical games show that constraint can expose the core verb

### Observation
Program Pochette preserved games often reduce play to one motion rule, one resource, or one spatial transformation: steering a missile, redirecting a ball, surviving with constrained movement, sliding puzzle pieces, moving carpets while avoiding enemies.

### Mechanism
Technical constraint forces the game to depend on a small number of readable state transitions rather than content volume.

### Generalized Principle
**Prototype a system in a deliberately reduced form to test whether its core verb generates decisions before adding production complexity.**

### Transfer
Before building full sports presentation, test passing/space with shapes; before full city generation, test road-choice consequences with abstract graphs.

### Playtest Question
With art, progression and spectacle removed, does the core verb still create anticipation, correction or planning?

# Promotion candidates

Strong enough for future Core Principles promotion:
- Camera changes are control-system changes.
- Team AI should be evaluated at group-intent level.
- Important choices need observable evidence of remembered state.
- Irreversibility and experimentation are separate axes.
- Restart latency is part of challenge difficulty.
- Automate obvious execution, preserve strategic ownership.
- Automation needs visible intent and override points.
- AI personality should come from decision priorities before hidden bonuses.
- AI presentation bandwidth limits usable complexity.
- Automated agents should cover repetitive validation; humans judge feel.

- separate readable scenario variation from movement/collision determinism

- delayed narrative choices should receive immediate acknowledgment
- distinguish commitment choices from experimentation choices
- automation for inventory/crafting must expose what it plans to consume/do
