# A. Procedural city / road generation

## A1. Road hierarchy comes before surface detail

Road width, intersection density and branching logic should encode transport roles.

### Principle
**Generate a navigable transport graph first; decorate it as a city second.**

---

## A2. Connectivity is necessary but insufficient

A graph can be mathematically connected yet unpleasant to drive.

Additional checks:
- curve radius
- slope
- repeated braking
- visibility
- short intersection spacing

### Principle
**Add driveability tests after connectivity tests.**

---

## A3. Rapid automation should increase design iteration

Procedural tooling that can turn curves into near-final streets quickly lets designers compare many layouts before art lock.

### Principle
**Use automation to multiply design experiments, not only content volume.**

---

## A4. Landmarks are navigation infrastructure

Procedural cities need persistent spatial anchors.

### Principle
Use silhouette, height, color and district grammar to help the player know where they are without constantly opening a map.

---

## A5. LOD must preserve navigation-critical structure

Road disappearance, intersection shifts or landmark loss at distance directly damages driving decisions.

### Principle
**Navigation-critical geometry has a higher LOD priority than decorative detail.**

---

## A6. Seed alone may not be enough

Generator updates can change output for the same seed.

### Principle
Store:
- seed
- generator version
- preset
- generation trace.

---

# B. Replay / spectator

## B1. Replay is infrastructure, not only presentation

Replay can support:
- bug reproduction
- ghost racing
- kill cam
- highlights
- spectator playback.

### Principle
**Plan reproducibility early if several of these systems are likely.**

---

## B2. Determinism failures amplify over time

Tiny ordering or timing differences can create entirely different outcomes later.

### Principle
During development, compare state at checkpoints to locate the first divergence.

---

## B3. Replay has multiple fidelity targets

Visual particles may differ without issue in a highlight replay, while score/state differences are unacceptable in competitive validation.

### Principle
**Define which state must reproduce for each replay use case.**

---

## B4. Kill cam can close the learning loop

Showing where an attack came from and what preceded it converts death into information.

### Principle
**Replay the causal chain, not merely the final animation.**

---

## B5. Spectator clarity benefits players too

A viewer entering mid-match needs the same basic legibility a new player needs:
- current state
- danger
- objective
- likely next event.

### Principle
**Treat spectator UI as a continuous tutorial.**

---

## B6. Broadcast needs focal management

Large simultaneous fights can exceed a viewer's attention capacity.

### Principle
Provide objective cameras, tactical views, free camera and player-follow options.

---

# C. Touch ergonomics

## C1. Touch is not a controller without plastic

Lack of tactile feedback makes small virtual buttons and sticks harder to use without looking.

### Principle
**Use larger regions, direct touch and dynamic placement before shrinking a gamepad onto glass.**

---

## C2. Direct interaction is a native strength

Touching an object can be more natural than steering a cursor toward it.

### Principle
Translate game intent into the device's natural gestures.

---

## C3. Finger occlusion is part of the camera/UI budget

The hand literally covers the playfield.

### Principle
Track the physical thumb footprint as an occluder during playtests.

---

## C4. Port decisions, not button count

Complex console games can remain strategically deep on touch if multiple physical actions are remapped into context-sensitive direct gestures.

### Principle
**Preserve choice complexity, not hardware complexity.**

---

## C5. Invisible controls need optional input visualization

HUD-free controls can still expose direction/amplitude through temporary arrows or rings.

### Principle
Minimal UI should not make input state mysterious.

---

# D. UGC ecosystem

## D1. Discovery is part of creation

An editor with no discovery system produces invisible work.

### Principle
The complete loop includes publish, discover, play, feedback and revision.

---

## D2. Popularity-only ranking creates lock-in

Existing winners receive more exposure and new creators struggle to get initial data.

### Principle
Mix popular ranking with new, personalized, curated and exploratory surfaces.

---

## D3. First impression metadata matters

Players often decide in seconds whether to try a UGC item.

### Principle
Expose enough structured metadata to set expectations before launch.

---

## D4. Recommendation errors hurt both sides

A bad match wastes player attention and denies the creator a relevant audience.

### Principle
UGC recommendation quality should be evaluated for creator health as well as player clicks.

---

## D5. Moderation starts in the data model

Text, image, audio, ghosts and other player-shared data can all become moderation surfaces.

### Principle
Design reporting, blocking and age restrictions before scale arrives.

---

## D6. Freeform input should earn its moderation cost

If a preset tag can deliver the same product value as arbitrary text, controlled vocabulary may be preferable.

---

## D7. Creators need analytics to improve

Views alone are not enough.

Useful:
- starts
- completion
- quit location
- retries
- ratings.

---

# E. Long-term comeback

## E1. Positive framing changes identical math

A reduced XP rate during continued play and a Rested XP bonus after time away can be mathematically equivalent but emotionally different.

### Principle
**Turn absence penalties into positive comeback bonuses where possible.**

---

## E2. Return systems should reduce re-entry cost

A returning player may have forgotten controls and goals even if their character is strong enough.

### Principle
Catch-up includes knowledge recovery, not only numeric power.

---

## E3. Comeback should not erase veteran value

Help returning players reach playable content while preserving mastery, collections and records earned by consistent players.

---

## E4. Multiple progression clocks reduce obligation

Not all progress needs daily cadence.

Combine:
- permanent mastery
- optional daily
- seasonal track
- rested progress.

---

## E5. Return triggers are invitations

Notifications, social commitments and timed opportunities can invite return, but severe absence penalties convert them into obligations.

### Principle
**Design a reason to come back, not fear of staying away.**
